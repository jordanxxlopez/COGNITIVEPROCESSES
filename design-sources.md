# Appearance references

The study material and lecture images are unchanged. White and black controls below Individual collections select persistent light and dark themes. Expanded-view notes start at one line and grow with their content, with manual vertical resizing retained.

The existing purple accent is preserved. No claim is made that purple, green, or dark mode treats ADHD or improves every user's learning.

- W3C, *Making Content Usable for People with Cognitive and Learning Disabilities*: consistent visual design, helping users focus, avoiding distractions, and supporting adaptation and personalization. https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/
- W3C, *Understanding SC 1.4.3: Contrast (Minimum)*: normal text requires at least 4.5:1 contrast for WCAG AA. Tested dark-theme teaching text, emphasis, captions, note text, supporting note labels, and tags exceed that threshold. This is a targeted color check, not a claim of complete WCAG conformance. https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html
- *Dark mode vogue: Do light-on-dark displays have measurable benefits to users?* The reported effects depend on age, environment, task, and preference; negative polarity was not universally better. These are general display findings, not ADHD-specific treatment evidence. https://doi.org/10.1080/00140139.2022.2160879

Themes preserve correct/incorrect text and symbols alongside color. No animation was added. Original lecture imagery is not inverted or recolored.
