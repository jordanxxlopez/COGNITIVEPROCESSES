## Page 2 · Collecting association norms
Slide image: ling406/module1/assets/lecture04/002.webp

### Original slide text
Association Norms:
We’ve read a little about these.
For your first taste of what it’s like to be a participant in one of these studies (or maybe 
not if you’ve done research for extra credit before) we’ll do some “norming” in class.
I’ll put a word on the screen for a few seconds, and you write down the first few words 
that come to mind.

### Existing reviewed notes (PSYC301 v20-style edition), for facts only, do not copy the wording
- **Association norms** summarize the words that people produce in response to a cue. A participant sees a word briefly and writes the first few words that come to mind. Collecting responses across people reveals which associations recur and which responses are unusual. The task asks for immediate associations rather than a dictionary definition. Its outcome is a pattern of responses to each cue, so the same word can elicit several different associates across participants.
- An association is measured through a response. Presenting the same cue to multiple people makes it possible to compare their responses and identify common connections between words.

- The explanatory goal is to specify what a representation does. Its activity, its connections and the way those connections change determine how input can influence a response.
