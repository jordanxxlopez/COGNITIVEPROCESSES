Today we’ll be talking about morphology and sentence structure. This

### Existing reviewed notes (PSYC301 v20-style edition), for facts only, do not copy the wording
- **A word form depends on its grammatical role.** Knives is the plural of the noun knife, but knifes is a valid present-tense form of the verb knife. In He knifes him, the singular subject requires a third-person singular verb. The apparent Scrabble disagreement disappears once the noun and verb uses are distinguished. Using knife as a verb is zero derivation, which creates a word in a different category without adding a derivational affix. Adding the final -s to that verb is inflection. The example therefore combines creation of a verb from a noun with the grammatical marking required by the sentence.
- The spelling knifes cannot be judged independently of its use. It is not the noun’s irregular plural, but it is the verb form required after he in the present tense. Derivation supplies the verb knife, and inflection supplies knifes for its role in that sentence.
  - The dialogue contrasts knife as an object with knife as an action. The final sentence includes a singular subject, an inflected verb, and an object, so the grammatical context explains the disputed ending.

- **The grammatical context can require a particular inflected form.** In an ordinary counting expression beginning with two, a following count noun must express plural number. ## Page 13 · Distinguishing derivation and inflection
Slide image: ling406/module1/assets/lecture03/013.webp

### Original slide text
Summary so far:
Both inflectional and derivational morphology:
create new (phonological) forms out of old parts 
change the meanings of base forms
Derivational morphology:
creates new words
is productive, but not always, and typically limited
Inflectional morphology:
changes how words interact (syntactically) with other words
is highly productive, but not always regular
is required by syntax

### Existing reviewed notes (PSYC301 v20-style edition), for facts only, do not copy the wording
- **Derivation creates lexical items, while inflection supplies grammatical forms used in syntactic contexts.** Both processes build forms from existing material and affect interpretation. They differ in the kind of change they make. A derived word can have a new category or a partly conventional meaning, whereas an inflected form expresses a grammatical distinction associated with the same lexical item. Productivity and regularity are separate issues. Derivational patterns may extend to new words while remaining restricted. Inflection can be broadly required by grammar while using irregular sound changes. The knife examples illustrate both distinctions within one family of forms.
- Identify what a morphological change accomplishes. If it creates a new lexical use, such as the verb knife from the noun, it is derivational. If it marks the form needed for a sentence, such as knifes after he, it is inflectional. Sound regularity alone cannot decide between them.
  - The comparison separates three points about inflection—syntactic interaction, broad productivity, and possible irregularity—from the creation and restricted productivity associated with derivation. These properties should be compared independently.
