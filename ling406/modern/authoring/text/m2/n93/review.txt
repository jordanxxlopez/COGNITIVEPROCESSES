54|The **Revised Hierarchical Model of Kroll and Stewart (1990, 1994)** explains interactions between **L1** and **L2** in terms of **spreading activation**. Spreading activation means that activating one representation partly activates the representations connected to it. >> The model connects an L1 vocabulary, an L2 vocabulary and a shared store of concepts. >> **Lexical links** join words across the two languages. **Conceptual links** join each vocabulary to meanings. >> L1 words have strong links to concepts. L2 words have weaker links to concepts. || Consider the **Marian and Spivey** task in **L2**. Russian and English bilinguals hear an English instruction to pick up the **speaker**. >> The concept for "speaker" partially activates the Russian word for "matches", which is **spichki**. >> Speaker and spichki are linked only by **sound overlap**. They begin with the same sounds, but speakers and matches share no meaning. >> The activity of spichki feeds back to the conceptual activation for "matches". This happens because L1 words are strongly linked to their concepts. >> Conceptual activation for "matches" leads to **eye movements toward the L1 competitor**, the matches. The listener still picks up the speaker. || Now consider the task done in **L1**. The conceptual links with L2 might be **too weak** to create this kind of interference reliably. >> An English competitor would need a strong link from its English name to its concept to draw the eyes during a Russian task. >> L2 words have only weak conceptual links in this model. || So interference runs from the first language into the second but not the other way.
