54|**Strong first-language links to concepts make Russian competitors draw looks during English listening, but weak second-language links do not work in reverse.** Kroll and Stewart (1990, 1994) built the **Revised Hierarchical Model** on **spreading activation** (activating one representation partly activates those linked to it). >> The model connects an L1 vocabulary, an L2 vocabulary and a shared store of concepts. >> **Lexical links** join words across the two languages. >> **Conceptual links** join each vocabulary to meanings. >> L1 words link strongly to concepts, and L2 words link weakly. || In the Marian and Spivey task in L2, Russian and English bilinguals hear an English instruction to pick up the speaker. >> The concept for "speaker" partially activates the Russian word for "matches", spichki. >> Speaker and spichki share only **sound overlap**, not meaning. >> The activity of spichki feeds back to the concept for "matches". >> Conceptual activation for "matches" leads to eye movements toward the L1 competitor. >> The listener still picks up the speaker. || In the task done in L1, the conceptual links with L2 might be too weak to create this interference reliably. >> An English competitor would need a strong link from its English name to its concept. >> L2 words have only weak conceptual links. >> So interference runs from the first language into the second, not the other way.
