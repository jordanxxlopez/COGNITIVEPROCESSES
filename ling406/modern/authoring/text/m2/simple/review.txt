54|Bilinguals listening in English look at the matches because the Russian word for matches gets partly activated and boosts its meaning. This is explained by the **Revised Hierarchical Model of Kroll and Stewart (1990, 1994)**. The model works by **spreading activation** (turning on one representation partly turns on the ones linked to it). || The model has three parts. >> One part is the **L1** vocabulary (the first language). >> Another part is the **L2** vocabulary (the second language). >> The third part is a shared store of concepts (meanings). >> **Lexical links** connect words across the two languages. **Conceptual links** connect each vocabulary to meanings. >> L1 words are strongly linked to concepts, and L2 words are weakly linked. || In the **Marian and Spivey** task in L2, the model works step by step. Russian and English bilinguals hear an English instruction to pick up the speaker. >> The concept for "speaker" partly activates the Russian word for "matches", which is spichki. >> This is because speaker and spichki start with the same sounds. They share **sound overlap** but no meaning. >> The activity of spichki feeds back and boosts the concept for "matches". >> The boosted concept for "matches" makes the eyes move toward the L1 competitor. The listener still picks up the speaker. || This does not happen when the task is done in L1. This is because the conceptual links for L2 words might be too weak. An English competitor would need a strong link from its English name to its concept. Since L2 words only have weak links, interference goes from the first language into the second but not back.
