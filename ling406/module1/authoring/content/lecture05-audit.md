## Scientific distinctions preserved

Phrenology's invalid skull measurement and poorly constrained psychological categories are distinguished from the general possibility of functional specialization. Flourens's findings are described with the lesion-extent issue that the lecture emphasizes.

Tan's restricted production is contrasted with apparently intact comprehension. Broca's case is not reduced to a precisely isolated surface speech center. Hughlings Jackson's distinction between locating damage that disrupts speech and locating speech itself is retained as substantive source content.

Page 9 explains that Dronkers and colleagues found deep injury including the insula and superior longitudinal fasciculus in the preserved historical brains. The association between speech apraxia and the insula is attributed to work by that group. It is not generalized into a universal claim that the insula alone produces speech or that Broca's region has no language role.

Lichtheim's A, B and M labels and the lowercase input/output pathways were checked against the image. A–B and A–M disruptions remain distinct comprehension and repetition predictions.

H.M.'s example is described as temporal-region surgery without reproducing the source's imprecise “middle temporal lobe” wording. No unprovided detailed diagnosis or surgical account was added.

Metabolic imaging is distinguished from direct neural measurement with the actual seconds-versus-milliseconds and millimeters-versus-microns comparisons. These are source-taught methodological relationships, not appended generic cautions. The imaging-resolution comparison is framed in the context of the early methods discussed.

The PET figure's embedded caption provides three important contrasts that ordinary text extraction misses. They are monitoring visual words for dangerous animals versus passive viewing, generating uses versus repeating visual words, and generating uses versus repeating auditory words. Teaching preserves these contrasts and the left inferior frontal overlap, with purple-to-white representing relative activity within a condition. Posner's component-operation conclusion is kept distinct from assigning an entire task to one area.
