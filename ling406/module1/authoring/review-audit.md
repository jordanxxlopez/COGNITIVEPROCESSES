# Midterm 1 review source audit

The supplied review has 56 pages. All seven review contact sheets were inspected, including every title and divider. Original source slides were compared in six mapping contact sheets, with additional full-size inspection of the activation graph, the Balota and Lorch abstract and table, the Belke abstract, and the Belke eye-movement and new-item graphs. The mapping decision uses both the visual and the extracted text. Similar extracted text alone was insufficient in the two Balota and Lorch cases below.

There are 43 verified content correspondences and 13 separately authored records. The original review image and original extracted text remain attached to every review page. A correspondence reuses an explanation for matching instructional content, rather than replacing the review image with the original lecture image. The original and review pages sometimes differ in aspect ratio, line breaks and wording, so these are not claims of pixel identity.

## Separately authored pages

- Pages 1, 3, 10, 17, 29, 35, 44 and 49 are title or theme divider pages. Their transcript is empty. Page 29 identifies Lichtheim and his house, but does not add an instructional claim. The supplied final theme is numbered 8, and that numbering is preserved.
- Page 2 describes the supplied review's ten short essay questions, 100–250 words per answer, three or four gradeable items, partial credit and open-book format. Its prose attributes the format to the supplied review rather than guaranteeing the format of another exam.
- Page 21 adds three candidate mechanisms for frequency effects to the activation dynamics graph. Its explanation distinguishes baseline activation, incoming connection strength and recognition threshold. The graph illustrates time-varying CAVE activation and does not plot a high-frequency/low-frequency comparison.
- Page 25 contains an image of the Balota and Lorch abstract. Lecture 4 page 29 is only the heading “A neat experiment...” and has no abstract in its rendered source. A text-only matcher would wrongly classify these as equivalent. The new explanation preserves the pronunciation versus lexical-decision result and the authors' task-specific interpretation.
- Page 26 has the same connected LION–TIGER–STRIPES illustration as Lecture 4 page 40 but reverses the verbal example. The review says LION primes STRIPES while the input label in the image remains STRIPES. The explanation identifies both the review's verbal example and the illustrated input without silently changing either. Both routes rely on TIGER as the intermediary.
- Page 27 contains the Balota and Lorch pronunciation latency table. Lecture 4 page 41 has the closing bullets but no table in its rendered source. The review receives its own explanation with 250 and 500 ms SOAs and mean latencies of 549 ms related, 558 ms mediated, 577 ms neutral and 575 ms unrelated. Mediated-versus-control differences of 19 ms and 17 ms were checked arithmetically.

## Image-only and quantitative content

Pages 50 and 52 have blank extracted text but contain the Belke, Meyer and Damian paper abstract. Both correspond to Lecture 9 page 14 and are instructional, not empty pages. The abstract was read from the image, including the first-cycle result, the naming and gaze-duration result, the absent blocking effect on gaze-to-speech lag, and new-item generalization.

Page 54's three graphs correspond to Lecture 9 page 26. Speech duration decreases with repetition without a blocking effect. Gaze duration shows the homogeneous disadvantage after the first cycle. The lag graph does not show a significant blocking effect, and its reversed numerical difference is not represented as a reliable benefit. Page 55 corresponds to Lecture 9 page 27. It introduces new items in cycle 5, where both conditions slow while the homogeneous disadvantage persists. The source-specific controls and the question about phonological encoding on page 56 are retained through Lecture 9 page 29.

## Study aids and checks

The review includes ten original multiple-choice questions, two written prompts, seven guide groups and four experiment groups. The written prompts target the functional/positional distinction and the converging evidence for semantic blocking. Every question and guide points to a review source page. Coverage validation checks that the authored and mapped sets are disjoint and jointly equal pages 1–56. Teaching prose was checked for the contract's banned narration, stock expressions, colons and semicolons.
