54|The **Revised Hierarchical Model of Kroll and Stewart (1990, 1994)** explains interactions between a first language, **L1**, and a second language, **L2**, in terms of **spreading activation**. Spreading activation means that activating one representation partly activates the representations connected to it. >> The model connects an L1 vocabulary, an L2 vocabulary and a shared store of concepts. >> **Lexical links** join words across the two languages, and **conceptual links** join each vocabulary to meanings. >> L1 words have strong links to concepts, while L2 words have weaker conceptual links. || Consider what happens in the model during the **Marian and Spivey** task in **L2**, when Russian–English bilinguals hear the English instruction to pick up the **speaker**. >> **The concept for “speaker” partially activates the Russian word for “matches,”** which is **spichki**. >> The link between speaker and spichki is **sound overlap**. The two words begin with the same sounds, so activity for speaker spreads to the similar-sounding Russian word. >> Speakers and matches share no meaning, so the competition comes entirely from the shared sounds. || **The activity of spichki feeds back to the conceptual activation for “matches.”** >> Because L1 words are strongly connected to their concepts, the partly active Russian word strengthens the concept it names. >> **Conceptual activation for “matches” leads to eye movements toward the L1 competitor**, the picture of matches. >> The listener can still pick up the speaker correctly. The looks to the matches reveal a competitor that was active along the way. || **When the task is done in L1**, the **conceptual links with L2 might be too weak to create this kind of interference reliably**. >> An English competitor would need a strong link from its English name to its concept to attract the eyes during a Russian task. >> L2 words have only weak conceptual links in this model, so the reverse effect is unreliable. >> The unequal strength of the links explains why the other language interferes in one direction but not the other.
