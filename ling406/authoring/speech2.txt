Klatt (1979, J Phon)
A very old and very general model of speech perception
On Monday, we discussed categorical 
perception.
The idea is that somewhere in the journey from 
the analog signal that comes in through the 
cochlea, and the activation of phonemes 
(segmental analysis), information is lost.  
This loss of information is helpful, because it 
allows us to make contact with higher levels of 
representation.
For example, we might assume that in order to 
identify words, we need to first identify their 
constituent phonemes.
Today we’ll talk about two problems for this 
simple view:
1. Cascaded processing, and
2. Feedback

Thus, this model explains word effects on phoneme category boundaries in terms of top-

phoneme level, producing greater activation for the phoneme consistent with a word.

===== PDF PAGE 25 =====
Categorical perception and the “lexical-phonetic interface”
As we saw last week, perception appears to be shaped by category boundaries.
Specifically: Given the same physical difference, it is more difficult to distinguish stimuli 
from the same category than from different categories.
Nothing we learned today really undermines that basic finding. 
BUT:
Ambiguity about phoneme identity does have an impact on word recognition. 
Word-level information seems to have an impact on phoneme categorization.
Thus, it can’t be the case that the word recognition system just sits there waiting for the 
phoneme recognition system to finish doing its job (like a lumber mill). Spoken word 
recognition seems to be more interactive than this.
