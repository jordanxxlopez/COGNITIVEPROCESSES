Course description

Psycholinguistics is an interdisciplinary field that focuses on how people learn, produce
and understand language. The specific goal of this course is to expose students to the
central topics, methods and major findings of psycholinguistics. We will discuss what is
known about language comprehension and production, and you will also learn about the
experiments and procedures that researchers use to investigate the psychological
processes that underlie them. Students will have the opportunity to get some hands-on
experience with data analysis and mini-experiments.

Course requirements

1. Readings. You are expected to read all assigned chapters and papers before the
class in which they will discussed, so that you can fully comprehend and participate in
class discussion. Classes are designed with the expectation that students have done the
reading beforehand.

2. Participation and in-class exercises. You are expected to participate in class
discussion by actively asking questions and being prepared to discuss the course
material. We will occasionally do brief in-class exercises and they will be graded on a
credit / no credit basis, and count towards the attendance and participation part of your
final grade. The activities aim to build on and extend the topics covered in class. They
also let the instructor keep an eye on students’ understanding of the concepts and ideas
covered in the course, to see if there are topics that need further explanation or
clarification.

3. Writing assignments. There will also be some writing assignments to be completed
outside of class time. These assignments will involve a range of tasks, such as
answering questions about the readings, interpreting data, or researching a topic on the
web or in the library. Your answers will be graded based on how thoroughly and carefully
the assignment was completed and what level of understanding and mastery of the
concept the answers reveal.


4. Exams. There will be two exams mid-term given in class on the dates noted below,
worth 20% of your grade, each. There will also be a cumulative final, focused (50% or
more) on the material in the final third of the course.

Grading scheme:

Midterm exams 60%

Final exam 25%

Homework and short assignments 15%

Statement on Academic Integrity

USC seeks to maintain an optimal learning environment. General principles of academic
honesty include the concept of respect for the intellectual property of others, the
expectation that individual work will be submitted unless otherwise allowed by an

instructor, and the obligations both to protect one’s own academic work from misuse by
others as well as to avoid using another's work as one’s own. All students are expected
to understand and abide by these principles. Scampus, the Student Guidebook, contains
the Student Conduct Code in Section 11.00, while the recommended sanctions are
located in Appendix A: http:/Awww.usc.edu/dept/publications/SCAMPUS/gow/. Students
will be referred to the Office of Student Judicial Affairs and Community Standards for
further review, should there be any suspicion of academic dishonesty. The Review
process can be found at: http://www.usc.edu/student-affairs/SJACS/.

We will talk about large language models, and especially their theoretical predecessors,
quite a bit in this course. Please do not use one to generate any text for the assignments
(with the obvious exception of illustrative examples clearly labeled as such). Writing is
thinking. If you are not writing your own assignments, you are not thinking about the
material, and not meeting the goal of the assignment, which after all, is not just to
generate some relevant text, but to think about the course material.
