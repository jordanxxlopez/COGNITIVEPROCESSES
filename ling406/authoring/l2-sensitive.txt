L2 learning as a model system for sensitive period research
Remember that Lenneberg (1967) developed an 
influential theory that the sensitive period had to 
do with the development of cerebral dominance, 
or developmental changes in language laterality.
This was motivated by
- a rough alignment of the literature on brain 
development with new insights about language 
milestones
- a review of the literature on resilience after 
brain injury or surgery


===== PDF PAGE 3 =====
L2 learning as a model system for sensitive period research
Much of what is known about L1 sensitive periods is from research on idiosyncratic 
cases, or relatively rare phenomena.
In contrast, many people learn second languages at different times in their lives, 
often with somewhat limited success. Those limits have also been used to reason 
about the timing of the sensitive/critical period.

The data
- strong relationship between AOA and accuracy
- early learners (up to about 8) are often near-perfect (native-like)
- nearly random distribution of accuracy for Late Arrivals


===== PDF PAGE 8 =====
Johnson & Newport argue that this is consistent with a sensitive period (in fact, they say 
“critical period”) for L2 learning.
First, the dependence of ulitmate attainment on AOA is consistent with a loss of language 
learning ability over development. 
(The timing is not entirely consistent with Lenneberg’s hypothesis, but remember that he 
was working with a large gap between early childhood and adolescence.)
Second, attainment in late acquisition is all over the place. 
After the closure of the sensitive period, they argue, attainment depends on idiosyncratic 
learning abilities, not the usual language acquisition processes.

I’m not sure we can even call these “conclusions”
Overall, L2 attainment is lower for older learners.
This depends on:
- what constructions are tested
- how they are tested
- language pairings
It is hard to dissociate:
- age of acquisition from amount/quality of experience
- transfer effects from L2 acquisition
- performance from competence, generally speaking
