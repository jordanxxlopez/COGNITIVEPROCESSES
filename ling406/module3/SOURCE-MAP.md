# Module III initial source map

This is an inventory based on extracted source text, not finished teaching or
validated figure interpretation. Original source visuals must be checked during
authoring, especially for pages with little extracted text.

| Collection | Content and evidence to develop |
| --- | --- |
| Reading 1 | Phonemes versus letters, orthographic depth, pseudoword decoding, ambiguity, regularity and frequency effects, interactive activation, the frequency-by-regularity interaction, acquired phonological and surface dyslexia, and the interpretation of double dissociations. Preserve concrete word and pseudoword stimuli when they explain the contrast. |
| Reading 2 | The Dual Route Cascaded model and its grapheme–phoneme conversion rules, modularity, PDP/triangle models, statistical consistency, phonological representations and nonword generalization, semantic impairment and exception-word reading, and model comparison. Examine the Woollams longitudinal evidence and the assumptions behind the Coltheart simulations rather than presenting model fit as decisive by itself. |
| Inflectional morphology | Wug testing, regular and irregular past tense, Words and Rules, U-shaped learning, the Rumelhart–McClelland model and its critiques, Joanisse–Seidenberg modeling, phonological versus semantic impairment, and specific language impairment. Preserve the source's terminology while distinguishing patient results from model manipulations. Figures on sparsely extracted slides require the PowerPoint visual render. |
| Language and thought | Linguistic relativity, relative/absolute/landmark spatial frames, the rotated spatial-array tasks, Li and Gleitman's testing-condition alternative, Levinson's memory-load/control comparisons, spatial metaphors for time, priming, reading-direction explanations, and button-response tasks. Distinguish a language-group association from evidence that isolates language as its cause. |
| Sign and Braille | Signed languages versus a tactile writing system, contrastive sign features and categorical perception, spatial grammar, lateralization, deafness versus signing experience, attention and motion-processing comparisons, sign versus tic-tac gestures, Braille, blindness onset, and blindfold/TMS evidence for cross-modal plasticity. Keep each participant group and comparison explicit. |
| Final review | Keep all 52 original review pages. Check each page against the five lecture collections, record verified matches, and author explanations of review-only content separately. Do not replace original review visuals with lecture visuals. |

Study emphasis should follow the supplied review plus the concepts needed to
understand its evidence. Core flags and experiment groups are intentionally
unset until that mapping is checked. A PDF's link to an audio/video example is
not an available recording. Do not invent a recording or describe unseen motion.

For each experimental page, record the source page, participants or modeled
system, task, manipulated conditions, outcome measure, result, interpretation,
and any alternative explanation actually included in the material. Use these
records to support teaching, experiment comparisons and questions consistently.
