## What the infants actually heard

Printed411–412, PDF5–6, “Stimuli” and Table1:

- Natural recordings, not synthetic speech. A female native Filipino speaker read monosyllables with a level intonational contour.
- Three different tokens each of [ma], [na], and [ŋa] were selected from a larger recording set, making nine original tokens.
- Selection minimized within/across-category differences in nasal/vowel F0, overall pitch contour, and the latter vowel quality. Numeric F0 values are not supplied in the table.
- An independent native Filipino adult listener, distinct from the recorded speaker, reliably identified all nine selected stimuli as intended.
- [ma]–[na] was the salient comparison; [na]–[ŋa] was the less salient comparison. The same three [na] tokens were used across comparisons and experiments.
- Each trial contains nine tokens, comprising the three tokens of one category repeated three times in random order. Two trial sets use the same tokens in different orders.
- Trials are approximately14s; within-trial inter-stimulus intervals approximately1000ms.
- Same-category test trials contain the same tokens in a different order. Change-category test trials contain tokens of the other category. This is not a simplistic repeated-single-token test.
- Experiment1 tests [ma]–[na], Experiment2 [na]–[ŋa], Experiment3 tests both contrasts at4–5months, Experiment4 tests the same [na]/[ŋa] tokens in infants exposed to Filipino or another Austronesian language of the Philippines.

## Directly relevant primary synthetic-nasal research

Larkey, L.S., Wald, J., Strange, W. (1978), “Perception of synthetic nasal consonants in initial and final syllable position,” Perception&Psychophysics23(4),299–312. DOI https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03199713 . Open primary PDF https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/BF03199713.pdf downloaded and relevant Methods/Results read. Private files /workspace/scratch/larkey1978.pdf/.txt.

Printed301/PDF3 describes Minnesota Glace-Holmes parallel-resonance synthesis. It generated a10-step nasal+/æ/ series varied only in F2/F3 transitions, with a dedicated low nasal resonance and independently controlled amplitudes. Listeners supplied actual phoneme identifications. This is a strong scientific methodological basis for synthetic nasal examples; it is not the exact Narayan [a] vowel or natural-recorded tokens.

Key parameters:

- Nasal resonance265Hz, beginning100ms before upper formants.
- F2 onset1048→2123Hz over10steps; F3 onset2113→2848Hz over first5steps then declines to finalstep.
- F2/F3 transitions40ms linear to1765/2480Hz.
- F1 steady720Hz begins40ms after upperformant onset.
- Nasal amplitude−9dB relative to fully developed F1 and decays over70ms from upper-formant onset.
- F1 rises to full amplitude20ms after its onset. F2/F3 reach full amplitude within10ms; vowel levels−2/−7dB relativeF1.
- The paper warns that poorly specified synthetic nasals can fail phonetic identification, so “scientific synthesis” alone is not independent validation of a newly selected token.

## Display recommendation

Distinguish the two pairs explicitly. A main [na]→[ŋa] playback contains no [ma]. A separately labeled [ma]→[na] comparison explains the easier control. Preserve /ŋ/ as an initial velar nasal with no added oral /g/ release, and use a common [a] vowel, voice, pitch contour and timing across the newly constructed syllables. New synthesis can match the desired syllable contrast and approximate published duration/formant constraints, but must not be represented as recovered original study audio or as having passed native-listener validation unless that validation actually occurs.
