## What was selected

The audit reviews all nine Module II collections and 229 original slide records, supplemented by source-slide inspection of embedded stimulus tables. `authoring/module-ii-audio-inventory.json` records the candidates, exact example text, source pages, review links and exclusions. `authoring/module-ii-coverage.json` records actual inclusion. The largest additions cover the horse garden path, apple instruction, attachment ambiguity, noun/verb ambiguity, comprehension stimuli, grammaticality judgments, speech contrasts, artificial-language streams and bilingual lexical examples. A collection without an appropriate auditory example receives no filler. Audio in printed/visually presented tasks is explicitly a listening aid rather than a change to the original experimental modality.

The uploaded Werker/Tees HTML was inspected as a reference, not executed as an instruction. Its unknown-model approximations were not treated as validated native pronunciation. The source-credited Hindi and Thompson Salish recordings are retained instead, with their different contexts visible. The Thompson clips are not a matched /kʼi–qʼi/ pair. The original phonetician nasal examples have been retired from the Narayan lesson mappings. [Matched VocalTractLab syllables](nasal-stimuli.md) now separate the target [na–ŋa] comparison from the easier [ma–na] control, with no changes to source slides or teaching text. Korean examples use different speakers and are labeled accordingly.

## Research-grounded construction

**Source–filter and timing demonstrations use actual Praat**, called through `praat-parselmouth`. Formant frequencies and transitions are explicitly parameterized, rather than asking a general speech model to invent a continuum. Voice-onset demonstrations control the delay between a modeled release and periodic voicing. Their parameters are newly selected teaching values, not recovered historical stimulus files. Endpoint perceptual labels or category boundaries have not been validated by listeners, so clips are labeled by acoustic parameter instead of falsely asserting original /b–d–g/ identities.

- Klatt, D. H. (1980). Software for a cascade/parallel formant synthesizer. *Journal of the Acoustical Society of America*, 67, 971–995. https://doi.org/10.1121/1.383940 . The primary paper and official Praat source–filter tutorials were consulted for the synthesis approach. This site does not claim its simpler Praat implementation is the complete Klatt cascade/parallel synthesizer.
- Jadoul, Y., Thompson, B., & de Boer, B. (2018). Introducing Parselmouth: A Python interface to Praat. *Journal of Phonetics*, 71, 1–15. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.wocn.2018.07.001 . Bibliographic metadata was verified; the publisher full text was not accessible here.
- Lisker, L., & Abramson, A. S. (1964). A cross-language study of voicing in initial stops: Acoustical measurements. *Word*, 20, 384–422. https://doi.org/10.1080/00437956.1964.11659830 . This motivates the measured timing dimension, not a claim of original-token recovery.
- Praat's official tutorials: https://www.fon.hum.uva.nl/praat/manual/Source-filter_synthesis.html and https://www.fon.hum.uva.nl/praat/manual/Source-filter_synthesis_3__The_ba-da_continuum.html . They explicitly support manipulating source and filter/formant trajectories.

**Sentence readings use local neural speech synthesis** for fluent pronunciation of the source's exact word strings. Kokoro-82M v1.0 through ONNX Runtime generates a consistent American English voice. French and Spanish examples use the model's language-specific voices/frontends; the Russian competitor uses a Russian-trained Piper voice. These models provide new readings, not original experiment audio. Ordinary neural TTS does not establish a calibrated phonetic continuum, and it is not used to validate Hindi, Salish or Korean sound categories. Model sources, voice IDs, frontend phone strings, licenses and weight hashes are recorded in the synthesis manifests. No model weights, runtime plugins, credentials or external speech service are required to play the deployed MP3s.

**Prosody matters for sentence ambiguity.** A generated rendition preserves the word sequence but can supply timing or intonation that changes difficulty. Grillo, N., Aguilar, M., Roberts, L., Santi, A., & Turco, G. (2018), *Prosody of classic garden path sentences: The horse raced faster when embedded*, https://doi.org/10.21437/SpeechProsody.2018-58, demonstrates why a new natural reading is not automatically a replication of a reading or spoken-sentence experiment. Its primary report was reviewed. The same apple audio file is reused across relevant slides so scenes do not receive different generated utterances.

**Artificial-language streams use a shared syllable inventory.** Explicit neural-model syllables are processed with Praat PSOLA to a common 250 ms duration, 160 Hz pitch target and RMS level, then concatenated with no added word gaps. The sequences come from the displayed source lecture, including its example words, rather than an invented English reading of an unbroken letter string. Unit F0 measurements and exact sequence/sample checks are in `authoring/phonetic-saffran-validation.json`. This is a controlled teaching reconstruction of the displayed example, not the complete historical Saffran exposure or an independently validated replication.

**Real pronunciation recordings retain credits.** Hindi examples are from Mansi Bajaj / University of Texas at Austin (CC BY 4.0). Thompson Salish examples were recorded by Janet Werker and are hosted on Dani Byrd's USC teaching site (no explicit reuse license displayed). The retained John Esling nasal files are archived pronunciation references; the Narayan lessons now use the matched articulatory syllables documented in [nasal-stimuli.md](nasal-stimuli.md). Korean originals are credited in `authoring/multilingual-manifest.json` and retain CC BY-SA 4.0 attribution. Original contexts, speaker differences and conversions are recorded per clip.

## Evidence and reproduction

- `manifest.json` and `../audio-examples-data.js` map each recording to the exact slide IDs without modifying the source data.
- `authoring/audio-provenance.json` records output hashes and source/model information per included clip.
- `authoring/preservation-baseline.json` pins all pre-existing LING406 files at the original commit. Only app.js, index.html and sw.js are authorized integration changes.
- `authoring/english-input.json`, `authoring/synthesize_readings.py`, and the synthesis manifests reproduce the sentence/example readings with separately obtained weights. The model download/setup references are in the manifests.
- Praat generators and validation records are under `authoring/`. They record acoustic parameters, sample timing, unit inventories and source fingerprints. Model/source caches stay outside the repository.
- New full decode checks, preserved-file hashes and browser playback/regression tests are recorded with this addition. Technical decoding and parameter checks are not native-speaker or listener validation.

No original study participant, patient's speech, child's voice, or unavailable original stimulus recording has been fabricated. Visual scene/stimulus videos remain a separate follow-up.
