# Study Studio writing

Keep existing versions available when a user requests a new transcript edition.
Teach the content of the individual source slide in connected, complete sentences.
Explain source terminology and instructional stimuli directly. Exclude presentation
narration, roadmaps, decorative-image descriptions, stock caveats, and filler.
Do not use the phrase “need not” in authored study materials. Use direct wording
that fits the meaning, such as “does not have to” or “does not necessarily.”
For LING406 Versions 3 and later, avoid colons and semicolons in
transcript prose. Do not use
“lawful” as evaluative framing. Do not introduce earlier or later slides.
Preserve source documents verbatim and keep notes, read marks, and quiz state
compatible when changing the interface.

LING406 Version 4 retains source wording within the teaching prose. Each page
must equal or exceed Version 3 in visible word count and character count.
Preserve explanations and do not pad logistical pages to increase their length.

Every individual lecture collection must display every original source slide in
source order, including the opening title, chapter dividers, and logistical
pages. A title or other noninstructional slide may have an empty transcript.
Do not omit a slide because no transcript is needed, and do not fill such pages
with invented teaching text. Preserve all existing transcripts and page-based
notes/read keys when restoring missing slides.

In PSYC301 teaching transcripts, state the course content directly. Do not write
“the lecture says,” “the lecture gives,” “the lecture describes,” or similar
source narration. This restriction applies to authored teaching prose, not
original source slides or lecture titles/navigation labels.
