2|**Gestalt laws of perceptual organization** describe how separate pieces of visual input are grouped into organized wholes. **Good continuation** means that points along a straight or smoothly curving line are seen as belonging together. A line tends to be seen as following the smoothest path rather than abruptly changing direction.||The **law of Prägnanz**, also called **simplicity** or **good figure**, means that a pattern is seen in the way that gives it the simplest overall structure. The **law of similarity** means that things that look alike tend to be grouped together. Smooth continuity, simple structure, and shared appearance therefore provide different ways of organizing the same visual field.||Gestalt psychologists claimed that these laws are **intrinsic or innate—built in, not learned**. In that account, the organizing tendencies are already part of the perceptual system rather than rules an individual has to acquire from past experience.

10|The **inferential approach** combines two ideas: **Helmholtz’s proposal that we resolve ambiguities by the likelihood principle**, and **regularities in the environment providing information for how those ambiguities can be resolved**.||Given an **image as input** and **knowledge about regularities of the physical world**, perception selects **the most probable interpretation of the image**.||The **input** supplies visible evidence, while a regularity supplies knowledge about what usually causes such evidence. 11|**Thomas Bayes (1701–1761)** is associated with a formal account of probability updating. 12|For **Bayesian inference in hypothesis testing**, **P(H|E) = P(E|H)P(H) / P(E)**. **H** is a perceptual hypothesis and **E** is the image received.||**P(H)** is the prior probability of percept H. **P(E|H)** is the probability of seeing image E assuming H is the correct explanation. **P(E)** is the probability of seeing E regardless of a particular perceptual hypothesis.
