## p3
- A **neural network**, NN, assigns a 48-million-pixel face image to one of 1000 people, or a speech wave to one of 25 sounds. A pixel is one dot stored as a number, and a speech wave is a list of pressure numbers, so both problems have the same form, numbers in and a choice among fixed options out. The network is defined by its behavior. Its job is to take a bunch of numbers and return a **probability distribution**, a **pdf**, one probability per possible answer.
- With 5 vowels in the agreed order read, rad, rude, Rud, rod, a pdf might be [0.2, 0.2, 0.4, 0.2, 0]. The order matters, the probabilities must sum to 1 because the sound must be one of the five, and the best guess is rude at 0.4, with 60 percent of the chance spread elsewhere.

## p4
- The input is a **vector**, an ordered list of numbers such as [4, 1, minus 3, 2, 1, 0.4, 1, 2, minus 3, and so on], the pressure numbers of a speech wave, and the output is a decision pdf over the vowels. The network's **knowledge** is in **weights**, w, combined with the input through billions of calculations across several trillion weights. The weights are found by **"Fake it till you make it,"** FITYMI, carried out by **SGD**. Start with random weights, give the network the vowel in Rud, and in **forward propagation** it produces a wrong pdf such as [0.4, 0.1, 0.2, 0.2, 0.1]. The correct pdf is [0, 0, 0, 1, 0], the gap is the **error** or **loss**, the weights are adjusted to shrink the loss, and the loop repeats with another signal, billions of times. The wrong pdf still sums to 1, but the probability is in the wrong places, and learning moves it toward the target.

## p6
- **Santiago Ramón y Cajal** established that the brain is made of **neurons**, separate cells rather than one continuous web. In his retinal circuit drawing, circles are **somata**, cell bodies, each neuron has an **activity** or **activation**, and arrows are **synaptic contacts** labeled with a **weight w**, a signed number saying how strongly and in which direction one neuron's activity affects the next. Reduced to three inputs and one receiving neuron, the activations are minus 1, 1 and 3 and the weights are minus 2, 3 and 1. **One neuron computes a weighted sum.** Minus 1 times minus 2 is 2, 1 times 3 is 3, and 3 times 1 is 3, so the receiving neuron's activation is 8, with no **bias** added.
- This weighted sum is the **inner product**, **scalar product** or **dot product**. Two lists of the same length are multiplied position by position and the results are added into one number, a scalar. With continuous functions it becomes the integral of f times g. This is the answer to the inference question for one neuron.

## p7
- Three publications turned brain chemistry and electricity into arithmetic. In 1943, **Warren McCulloch and Walter Pitts** described nervous activity with logic and calculation. In 1962, **Frank Rosenblatt** developed the **perceptron**, an artificial neuron that computes a weighted sum and decides. In 1986, **David Rumelhart, James McClelland and the PDP Research Group** described cognition as many simple units working at once, with knowledge in the weights, and presented backpropagation. Over four decades, neural activity came to be described as numbers and synaptic signaling as multiplying by weights and adding, a modeling step rather than a claim that the materials are identical.

## p9
- A **multilayer perceptron**, MLP, is layers of artificial neurons where every neuron connects to every neuron in the next layer. The example has an **Input** layer, **Hidden 1, 2 and 3**, and an **Output** layer, with sizes 5, 3, 4, 3 and 7, so 15 plus 12 plus 12 plus 21, or **60 weights**. Every hidden neuron takes a dot product of the incoming activations and weights. With inputs 2, 1, 3, 4 and 2 and weights 2, minus 1, 1, 3 and minus 2, the middle neuron of Hidden 1 gets 4 minus 1 plus 3 plus 12 minus 4, or 14, and the other two get 4 and minus 3. Hidden 2 comes out as 6, minus 2, 5 and 1, and Hidden 3 as 3, minus 1 and 2.
- Passing activations layer to layer is **forward propagation**, the full answer to inference. These are **linear hidden activations**, plain weighted sums with no bias and no **nonlinear activation function**. The output layer's values, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.30, 0.15, 0.12 and 0.08, come from **softmax**, which turns raw scores into positive numbers summing to 1.00, the **decision pdf**, with the fourth answer at 0.30 the best guess.

## p12
- Learning by FITYMI starts with random weights and one random **input-output pair**, the input [minus 2, 1, 3, minus 4, 2] with the correct output [0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 1, 0]. Forward propagation returns a wrong pdf, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.30, 0.15, 0.12 and 0.08, with only 0.12 on the sixth answer and 0.30 on the fourth, and the difference is the loss. The network changes its weights to make its output closer to the true pdf using **SGD**, now called **backpropagation**, passing the output error backward through the layers and adjusting each of the 60 weights. One adjustment makes the network slightly less wrong, and the process repeats with **billions of I/O pairs** at **data centers**.

An estimate learned from this data is pulled toward the mistake, as a network trained on data with errors learns some of them. SGD sees the data one point at a time in random order, taken here as the listed order, and adjusts w after each point.
