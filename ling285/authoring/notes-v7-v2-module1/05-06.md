## p3
- A **harmonic oscillator** is built from two quantities. The quantity x is how far something is displaced from its resting point, and v is its **velocity**, how fast and in which direction x is moving. A dot over a variable is **Isaac Newton's notation** for its change over time, so ẋ and v̇ are the rates of change of x and v. A positive rate means the quantity is rising, a negative rate means it is falling, and the size of the rate is the speed of the change.
- The first rule is that ẋ equals v, so v steers x. The second rule is that v̇ equals minus x, and the **minus sign** means v is always pushed opposite to where x is. The defining feature is that **x and v change each other**, and that two-way dependence produces repeating back-and-forth motion.

## p4
- **Suppose v equals negative 5.** Then ẋ is negative 5, so x will go down, whatever the value of x. Suppose x equals negative 5. Then v̇ is minus negative 5, which is positive 5, so v will go up. The two minus signs cancel. This is the restoring behavior in action, since an upward v then lifts x back toward zero.
  - The positive cases read the same way. A v of positive 5 makes x rise, and an x of positive 5 makes v fall. These are statements about the direction of change, so a velocity that is going up may still be negative, moving from a large negative value toward zero.

## p5
- The two rules treat the sign differently. The rule ẋ equals v keeps the sign, so positive velocity makes position rise. The rule v̇ equals minus x reverses it, so positive position makes velocity fall. Whenever x moves away from zero, the second rule turns v around and the first rule carries x back, a **restoring relationship** that keeps the system swinging. The quantity changed by v̇ is velocity, so a positive position can go with a falling velocity while the object still moves toward larger positions.

## p6
- **Start with x at its highest value and v at zero.** Because x is positive, v drops below zero, so x starts to decrease, slowly at first and then faster as v grows more negative. When x reaches zero, v̇ is zero, v is at its most negative, and x crosses zero at its fastest. Below zero, v̇ turns positive, v rises back toward zero while x falls more slowly, and when v returns to zero x is at its lowest point and turns around. With x negative, v becomes positive, x climbs, v peaks as x crosses zero, and x returns to its maximum as v comes back to zero. Then the cycle repeats.
- Computing the system at separate moments gives points that connect into two smooth waves. The x wave starts at the top and the v wave starts at zero, dipping negative first. They have the same shape shifted by a quarter cycle, so v is at its extremes when x crosses zero and v is zero when x is at its extremes.

## p7
- A **dynamical system** is a set of variables with rules for how they change over time, and the x-v system is an **oscillation** or **vibration dynamical system**, a quantity repeatedly swinging around a middle value. The relationship is a closed **loop of influence**, one arrow from v to x for ẋ equals v and one from x back to v for v̇ equals minus x. A change in v changes x, which changes v again, so once set in motion the system keeps cycling with nothing outside pushing it. Each cycle reaches the same height and depth and takes the same time.

It is a simplification, but it captures the back-and-forth pattern they share.

## p10
- A thin slice of air in the **vocal tract** holds many particles, and its **pressure** depends on how close together they are. **High pressure** means very close, **low pressure** means very far apart, and the particles themselves stay the same in number and size. **Compression** squeezes them closer than the **equilibrium spacing** and **rarefaction** spreads them farther apart. One pair of neighbors sits at 100 percent of its equilibrium distance at equilibrium, about 56 percent at the strongest compression and about 141 percent at the strongest rarefaction, with real speech moving far less.
- The slice behaves like a harmonic oscillator. The spacing relative to equilibrium plays the role of x. Squeezed air pushes itself apart and stretched air pulls itself back, so any departure is pushed back toward equilibrium, the same restoring relationship as v̇ equals minus x.

## p11
- The slice's motion follows a cosine, so x equals the cosine of the phase. Here x is the **normalized expansion**, scaled so plus 1 is maximum rarefaction, 0 is **equilibrium** and minus 1 is maximum compression. The **phase** is the position within a cycle, each 2π is one full cycle, and 0 to 6π covers three cycles. At phase 0 the cosine is plus 1, at π over 2 it is 0, at π it is minus 1, at 3π over 2 it is 0 again, and at 2π it is back at plus 1. Values along the way such as plus 0.74, minus 0.54, plus 0.31 and minus 0.21 say by their sign whether the slice is rarefied or compressed.
  - This is the same wave the x-v system produces when x starts at its maximum and v at zero, so the vibrating air in the vocal tract follows the harmonic oscillator relationship.

## p12
- Vowels differ in where the **tongue** sits, as seen in MRI side views with the speaker facing left. They are organized by **height**, **high** or **low**, and by **frontness**, **front**, **back** or **central**. **"Dean"** is high and front, with a large air space behind the tongue. **"Dune"** is high and back. **"Dan"** is low and front. **"Don"** is low and back, narrowing the space behind the tongue. **"Done"** is central. Each tongue position gives the air a different shape, and a differently shaped column of air vibrates differently, which is what makes the vowels sound different.

## p13
- A vowel is a sum of vibrations at **several frequencies at once**, so it can be modeled as **harmonic oscillators added together**. **Frequency** is cycles per second in **hertz**, and a vibration at f hertz is the cosine of 2π times f times t. The vowel /i/ in "dean" is modeled as 1 Hz plus 4 Hz, the cosine of 2πt plus the cosine of 8πt, a slow wave with fast ripples. The vowel /a/ in "Don" is modeled as 2 Hz plus 3 Hz, the cosine of 4πt plus the cosine of 6πt, whose close frequencies give a different pattern of tall and short swings.
  - These are example values with **equal amplitudes**, so each sum ranges between plus 2 and minus 2 and reaches plus 2 at 0, 1, 2 and 3 seconds. In between the components partly cancel or reinforce, so /i/ can be at minus 1.21 while /a/ is at plus 0.58. Positive x still means rarefaction and negative x compression, and the two time patterns are what distinguish the vowels.

## p15
- Two things vibrate during speech, the air in the vocal tract and the **vocal folds**. The folds are two folds of tissue in the **larynx**. Air from the lungs pushes them apart, the pressure drops as air rushes through, and they come back together, many times per second. Each opening lets through a puff of air, so the folds chop the airflow into rapid puffs that set the vocal tract air vibrating, and the tongue's shaping of the tract sets which frequencies it vibrates at. A vowel is one oscillator driving another, moving tissue and changing air, and an explanation of speech must account for both.

## p16

The distance of output above or below the trend plays the role of x, falling back from above and rising back from below.

## p19
- At the scale of atoms the harmonic oscillator appears as the **quantum harmonic oscillator**, whose energy is **quantized**, taking only certain levels. Matter is made of atoms with different numbers of electrons, some vibrating at low frequency and some at high, and electrons are **wave modes** rather than balls on tracks.
- **Magnesium** has 12 **electrons** and 12 **protons**, with ground-state configuration 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s². The older **Bohr-style shell** drawing puts electrons on circular paths in shells of 2, 8 and 2. The **quantum orbital** description adds the **electron density** of each occupied orbital, 1s², 2s², 2p⁶ and 3s², into a total density, darker where an electron is more likely. Orbitals are **quantized wave modes**, but magnesium is not a harmonic-oscillator atom.

The program uses numpy arrays, dt of 0.01 and 1000 steps, loops the two update lines, and plots x and v against t. The output reproduces the oscillator, with x tracing a cosine from the top and v starting at zero, dropping negative first, a quarter cycle behind.
