## p3
- A **dynamical system** is a system whose state changes over time according to rules. The course meets them in **animal behavior**, such as an octopus defending its home or a person speaking, and in **AI learning**, where a program changes its internal numbers as it is trained.
- Every dynamical system has three parts. The **elements** are the pieces, the **relations** are how the pieces are connected and coordinated, and the **goal** is what the whole system works to achieve. The same three parts describe an animal's body, a vocal tract or a learning program.

## p4
- An octopus defending its den is a dynamical system in behavior. Its **elements** are its eight **arms** and the **ground**. Its **relations** are the coordinated connections between each arm, the ground and the fish, **planned by a neural system** in its head and throughout its body, so control is not from one central point. Its **goal** is to **keep the den**.
> The octopus's elements, relations and goal | slide | 0

## p5
- Human speech is a dynamical system with many more elements. The **elements** are the **many parts of the tongue**, the **lips**, the **velum**, the **larynx**, the **palate** and the **pharyngeal wall**. The tongue counts as many parts because its tip, body and root move somewhat independently.
  - The **velum** is the soft back of the roof of the mouth, which raises to close off the nose or lowers to open it. The **larynx** is the voice box holding the vocal folds. The **palate** is the hard roof of the mouth, and the **pharyngeal wall** is the back wall of the throat.
- The **relations** are **planned by the brain**, so that producing a word moves the lips, tongue, velum and larynx at precisely coordinated times. The **goal** is to **convey a thought**. The MRI recording shows these parts moving together continuously over about three seconds of speech.

## p6
- All daily voluntary actions, and the involuntary actions of the heart, pancreas and neurons, are dynamical systems. Their elements range from **organs** down to **cells** and the **chemicals** inside them, so **biology, chemistry and physics** all study dynamical systems. The **speech system** is a very complex one.
- Because there are so many dynamical systems, one can be used to study another. An octopus arm and a human tongue look extremely different yet share an **underlying similarity** in how their elements, relations and goals are organized. This is **going up Diotima's ladder**, from individual cases to the general principles they share.

## p7
- The octopus example comes from the 2023 Marine Biology paper by **Jean Alupay, Jennifer Mather and Khalil Iskarous** on the octopus arm **slap**. An **Abdopus** octopus filmed in tidal pools in **Okinawa, Japan**, slapped at fish, and 19 slaps were measured in an **external reference frame** and an **animal-centered reference frame**.
- Each slap was built from two **kinematic primitives**, **rotation** and **translation** of the **hydrostatic** arms, combined **serially** and **in parallel**. A **muscular hydrostat** has no bones and is supported by muscles pushing against each other, so the arm can bend anywhere. Combining a few primitives keeps movement flexible while controlling only a few **degrees of freedom**, which the authors call **flexible rigidity**.
  - The slap supports **Flash and Hochner's embodied organization** view and the idea that motor primitives combine **syntactically**, by rules, as words combine into sentences. The octopus pairs **sensory feedback** about the moving fish with **feed-forward** primitives to stay in **dynamic equilibrium** with its environment.

## p8
- **AI** is a model of five animal psychological skills, **knowing, recognizing, attending, acting** and **learning**. Knowing is stored information, recognizing is identifying what arrives, attending is focusing on what matters, acting is producing an output, and learning is changing knowledge from experience.
- The **cloze procedure**, introduced by **Wilson Taylor** in Journalism Quarterly in Fall 1953, measured one of these skills in humans by removing words from a passage and having readers fill the blanks. Predicting a word from its context is the skill modern language AI performs.

## p9
- In **GPT** each word is a **vector** of thousands of numbers, called an **x** or a **token**. All of the AI's knowledge sits in a few trillion numbers called **weights**, or **w's**. The AI combines the W's with the x's using mostly **addition, subtraction, multiplication and division**, and the result is a **probability distribution function** over next words, with its tallest peak over **"quack"** for "chickens cackle and ducks."
- How the W's combine with the x's is itself a dynamical system. Learning is a simpler one, so it comes first.

## p10
- The weights come from training that starts with no knowledge, **"Fake it till you make it!"** The system starts with **random W's**, is given "chickens cackle and ducks," and predicts the next word. With random weights the peak may sit over "cat." The **target distribution** puts all the probability on "quack," so the system is told to **change its W's** toward it. Repeated over more sequences **billions of times**, this is how LLMs learn and know.

## p12
- The average of 10, 12 and 14 is easy, 36 divided by 3 gives 12, but that formula does not work for **speech, language, biological molecules and scenes**. The AI method starts with a **wild guess** x and improves it by exposure to the data, moving x a bit closer to each observed number in turn. This is **observation-based error learning**, the same logic as correcting random weights toward the right next word.

## p27
- SGD has two steps, make an initial guess and use data to improve it. **Stochastic** means a random observation is used. **Gradient** means the loss gives the direction, down for a positive discrepancy and up for a negative one. **Descent** means reducing the mistake. The path jitters, but its tendency is toward the average, and the same method applied to trillions of weights is how LLMs learn.

## p28
- A second data set, [62, 70, 78, 82, 90, 98], with average 80, makes the link between estimate and loss easier to see. The **mean motion of SGD** replaces the random observation with the average, so each new estimate is the old one minus 25 percent of its distance from 80. The **loss** is one half of the discrepancy squared, positive on either side of 80, and its rate of change as x changes is the discrepancy itself, which is why the discrepancy is the **gradient**.
- Starting at 140 the estimate goes 125, 113.75 and onward while the loss goes 1800, 1012.5, about 569.5 and toward zero. Each update keeps 75 percent of the discrepancy, the curve is smooth, and the **estimate goes down as the loss goes down**.

## p30
- Runs of 300 **epochs** from initial conditions of 130, 80 and 10 all settle around 80, and the learning rate decides how. At **10 percent** the runs reach 80 within 25 to 50 epochs and wiggle between about 75 and 85. At **50 percent** they arrive within a few epochs and then jump between about 70 and 95. At **1 percent** they move slowly and smoothly and are still short of 80 after 300 epochs. At **80 percent** they arrive at once and then jump between about 68 and 98. The initial condition affects only the start of each run.
