2|**A neuron converts a list of numbers into one weighted sum, and a neural network combines many of these calculations into a decision vector.** Each input is multiplied by its weight, and the products are added through an inner product. >> The network’s last vector contains a response for each possible answer. || **A language model takes a sequence of vectors and predicts what comes next.** Its layers transform those vectors until the output gives a probability distribution over the next token. >> The example predicts the sixteenth item from fifteen input vectors. || **An agentic system connects whole language models into a graph.** Different models plan, draft, criticize, revise and finalize an answer, with a loop allowing the critic to evaluate a revision again. >> The agents communicate in English and can also work in other human languages and computer languages. || **Inference processes an input with learned weights, while learning changes those weights.** Inner products support inference, and stochastic gradient descent uses backpropagation’s dynamic programming to organize learning.
3|**A language model develops its representations through many layers because next-word prediction is difficult.** A layer transforms the input vectors, and the following layer starts from those transformed vectors. >> This gives successive processing stages a chance to develop information useful for prediction. || **Several heads work in parallel within each layer.** Each head contributes as an expert, and the layer brings their contributions together into improved representations. >> The heads are components within the model, while an agentic graph connects whole language models. || **The next layer repeats this pattern with the representations it receives.** Its heads contribute in parallel again, and their combined result is passed onward. >> Heads provide different contributions within one stage, while layers develop the vectors across successive stages.
4|**A dynamical system repeatedly uses its current state to compute the next state.** In the oscillator, position and velocity take turns changing. 5|**A language model takes a sequence of vectors and predicts the next item.** A vector is a list of numbers, so the same input format can represent different kinds of material. >> The prediction task stays the same even when the sequence comes from a different source. || **The vectors can represent words in a sentence.** In the cat that the dog chased ran up the old tree and sat on the blank, the task is to predict the missing next word. >> The prediction uses the sentence so far, including the fact that the cat is the one that sat. || **The vectors can represent pieces of sound or a picture.** A spectrogram is divided into consecutive frames, and the model predicts the next frame of sound. A picture is divided into patches read row by row, and the task is to predict the missing bottom-right patch. >> The input represents an unfolding sound in one case and an ordered set of picture pieces in the other. || **The vectors can also represent amino acids in a protein.** The insulin B-chain example gives a sequence of amino acids and leaves the following residue to be predicted. >> The amino acid at the next position is Y. || **Words, sounds, picture patches and amino acids reach the model as sequences of vectors.** The model uses the preceding vectors to predict the following one, even though the original materials are different.
6|English allows only **certain sequences of sounds** in its words, and knowing which sequences are allowed is called **phonotactic knowledge**. **A simple syllable in English can have a consonant, then a vowel, then a consonant**, as in Don, mat, sit, cup, dog and fish. >> The pattern is about sounds, not letters, so the silent e in shave, kite and rope does not count, and two letters can spell one consonant sound, as in ship, chin and bath. || Syllables can also have **clusters**, groups of consonants together. A cluster before the vowel is an **onset**, as in split, flat and black, and a cluster after the vowel is a **coda**, as in desk, help and lamp. >> Each real word has an impossible twin marked with a star, and many twins use the same letters in the other order, such as flat and lfat, help and hepl, and lamp and lapm, so order matters. >> **The order mirrors around the vowel.** In flat the l sits right next to the vowel with the f farther out, and in help the l again sits next to the vowel, now on the other side, with the p farther out. Each impossible twin puts the wrong consonant against the vowel. || **A long word is a chain of syllables**, such as ba-na-na, At-lan-tic and ath-lete. The sounds t and l are fine across a syllable break, but tl cannot start a syllable, which is why Atlantic is a word but tlantic is impossible. >> The sound ng can end a syllable, as in ring, but it can never start one. >> So whether a sequence of sounds is allowed depends on where the syllable breaks fall.
7|**Phonotactic knowledge can be revealed through GPT learning.** Each item is the start of an English word, and the task is to guess the next letter. People agree on their guesses quickly, and how much they agree changes from item to item. || **After a vowel, almost anything can follow.** After ra, likely letters include t, n, r and l, and many others are possible too, because once a vowel has come the onset is over. >> **At the start of a word, the choices narrow sharply** to exactly what an English onset allows. After sp the next letter is l, r or a vowel and never n, and after k it is a vowel or n, as in knee. >> After b and f it is r, l or a vowel, after sh it is a vowel or r, as in shrimp, and after skr or m at the start of a word only a vowel can come. >> After bram, the letter b is almost forced, as in bramble, though a new syllable could also start there. || **Guessing the next letter is predicting the sixteenth item**, the same next-token game an LLM plays. A GPT trained only on this game learns English sounds and spelling, because its predictions can only get good by matching the patterns of English syllables. >> Its prediction is a probability distribution over the next letter, and the shape of that distribution carries the knowledge. >> After ra the distribution is broad, spread over many letters, while after skr it is sharply peaked on a few vowels, because English allows nothing else there. >> The more the letters so far restrict what can come next, the more the probability piles onto a few letters.
