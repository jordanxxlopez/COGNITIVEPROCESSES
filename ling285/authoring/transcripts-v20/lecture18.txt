2|**A single neuron takes a list of numbers and produces one number.** It multiplies the inputs by their weights and adds the products, which is an inner product. A neural network combines many such calculations, so one vector produces a decision vector with a response for each possible answer. >> The example network has layers of four, three, four and five units linked by three sets of weights. || **An LLM takes many vectors and predicts what comes next in the sequence.** Fifteen vectors pass through five layers, and the output is the sixteenth vector, which gives a probability distribution over the next token. >> The layers progressively change the representations, so the prediction uses the processed sequence rather than a single original input vector. || **An agentic system is a graph of LLMs that communicate with one another.** An Outline model plans, a Write model drafts, a Critic model evaluates, a Revise model changes the draft, and a Final model produces the answer. >> The critic and reviser are connected in a loop, so a revision can return for another evaluation. The agents communicate in English, while the models can also work in other human languages and computer languages. || **Inference uses the weights to process an input, while learning changes the weights.** The common operations are inner products for inference and stochastic gradient descent with dynamic programming for learning. >> Backpropagation organizes the backward calculation used to adjust the weights.
3|**Predicting the next word is difficult, so a language model processes its inputs through many layers.** Each layer changes the vectors into representations that can help make the prediction better, and the following layer takes those representations as its own starting point. >> A representation is the information about an input item carried by its vector. || **Each layer also contains several heads working in parallel.** They are described as different experts because they contribute different information to the processing within that layer. Their contributions are brought together to make the layer’s new representations. >> These heads are parts of the language model, rather than the separate LLMs connected into an agentic system. || **The process happens again at the next layer.** Its heads work alongside one another, their contributions are combined, and the resulting vectors continue onward through the network. >> So heads and layers solve different organizational problems. The heads contribute in parallel within a processing stage, while the layers form successive stages that develop the representations further.
4|**A dynamical system takes numbers, applies a rule, and feeds the result back in.** The oscillator begins with position one and velocity zero, and the rule determines how those values change step by step. >> First position is updated using the old velocity. Velocity is then updated using the new position, so the two quantities take turns rather than being updated independently from the old state. || **Repeating that rule builds the oscillator’s trajectory.** Position follows a cosine-like curve and velocity follows a shifted, minus-sine-like curve. >> The step size determines how far the calculation advances each time. The point is how a rule builds changing behavior from an initial state, rather than calculating every plotted value. || **Jordan’s recurrent network applies feedback to speech production.** The plan and state units feed the hidden layer, which produces output, and a copy of that output returns to the state units. >> Their self-connections also keep a fading trace of the previous state. The same weights are reused at successive moments, so what the system has just produced influences what it produces next. || **An LLM develops its vectors through successive layers, so depth plays the role of time.** Following one value upward through the five layers gives a changing curve, even though it is not a cosine. >> Each layer has its own weights, whereas the oscillator and Jordan network reuse the same rule at each time step. The shared idea is that a new state is computed from the state produced just before it. || The small stock-return graph traces Small, Medium and Large series over the same period. They fluctuate, rise and fall over the years, giving another example of quantities changing over time.
5|**A language model takes a sequence of vectors and predicts the next item.** A vector is a list of numbers, so the same input format can represent different kinds of material. >> The prediction task stays the same even when the sequence comes from a different source. || **The vectors can represent words in a sentence.** In the cat that the dog chased ran up the old tree and sat on the blank, the task is to predict the missing next word. >> The prediction uses the sentence so far, including the fact that the cat is the one that sat. || **The vectors can represent pieces of sound or a picture.** A spectrogram is divided into consecutive frames, and the model predicts the next frame of sound. A picture is divided into patches read row by row, and the task is to predict the missing bottom-right patch. >> The input represents an unfolding sound in one case and an ordered set of picture pieces in the other. || **The vectors can also represent amino acids in a protein.** The insulin B-chain example gives a sequence of amino acids and leaves the following residue to be predicted. >> The amino acid at the next position is Y. || **Words, sounds, picture patches and amino acids reach the model as sequences of vectors.** The model uses the preceding vectors to predict the following one, even though the original materials are different.
6|English allows only **certain sequences of sounds** in its words, and knowing which sequences are allowed is called **phonotactic knowledge**. **A simple syllable in English can have a consonant, then a vowel, then a consonant**, as in Don, mat, sit, cup, dog and fish. >> The pattern is about sounds, not letters, so the silent e in shave, kite and rope does not count, and two letters can spell one consonant sound, as in ship, chin and bath. || Syllables can also have **clusters**, groups of consonants together. A cluster before the vowel is an **onset**, as in split, flat and black, and a cluster after the vowel is a **coda**, as in desk, help and lamp. >> Each real word has an impossible twin marked with a star, and many twins use the same letters in the other order, such as flat and lfat, help and hepl, and lamp and lapm, so order matters. >> **The order mirrors around the vowel.** In flat the l sits right next to the vowel with the f farther out, and in help the l again sits next to the vowel, now on the other side, with the p farther out. Each impossible twin puts the wrong consonant against the vowel. || **A long word is a chain of syllables**, such as ba-na-na, At-lan-tic and ath-lete. The sounds t and l are fine across a syllable break, but tl cannot start a syllable, which is why Atlantic is a word but tlantic is impossible. >> The sound ng can end a syllable, as in ring, but it can never start one. >> So whether a sequence of sounds is allowed depends on where the syllable breaks fall.
7|**Phonotactic knowledge can be revealed through GPT learning.** Each item is the start of an English word, and the task is to guess the next letter. People agree on their guesses quickly, and how much they agree changes from item to item. || **After a vowel, almost anything can follow.** After ra, likely letters include t, n, r and l, and many others are possible too, because once a vowel has come the onset is over. >> **At the start of a word, the choices narrow sharply** to exactly what an English onset allows. After sp the next letter is l, r or a vowel and never n, and after k it is a vowel or n, as in knee. >> After b and f it is r, l or a vowel, after sh it is a vowel or r, as in shrimp, and after skr or m at the start of a word only a vowel can come. >> After bram, the letter b is almost forced, as in bramble, though a new syllable could also start there. || **Guessing the next letter is predicting the sixteenth item**, the same next-token game an LLM plays. A GPT trained only on this game learns English sounds and spelling, because its predictions can only get good by matching the patterns of English syllables. >> Its prediction is a probability distribution over the next letter, and the shape of that distribution carries the knowledge. >> After ra the distribution is broad, spread over many letters, while after skr it is sharply peaked on a few vowels, because English allows nothing else there. >> The more the letters so far restrict what can come next, the more the probability piles onto a few letters.
