3|**A harmonic oscillator is built from two elements, x and v, that change each other.** The interest is in how x and v change over time because of how they are related. A dot over a letter, which is Newton's way of writing it, stands for how fast that quantity is changing as time goes on. >> When the change of a quantity is positive, the quantity rises, and when its change is negative, the quantity falls. >> The two rules of the oscillator are that the change of x equals v, and that the change of v equals the negative of x.
4|An example makes the two rules concrete. If v is negative, then the change of x is negative, so x goes down. >> If x is negative, then the change of v is the negative of a negative, which is positive, so v goes up. >> So each element pushes on the other, and neither one ever stays still for long.
5|**Reading the two rules in words is what makes the oscillator easy to follow.** The first rule says that whenever v is positive x rises, and whenever v is negative x falls. The second rule says that whenever x is positive v falls, and whenever x is negative v rises. >> So x is always being pushed by v, while v is always being pushed back against x. >> That back-and-forth pushing is exactly what makes the system swing.
6|**Following the x and v system through time reveals an oscillation.** When x starts out negative and v starts at zero, the second rule makes v rise, and once v is positive, the first rule makes x rise toward zero. When x reaches zero, v is at its largest, so x keeps going and overshoots into positive values, and now the second rule pulls v back down. >> Once v becomes negative, x starts falling again, and the whole cycle repeats. >> So x traces a smooth wave that goes up and down forever, and v traces the same kind of wave, shifted so that it peaks whenever x is passing through zero.
7|**The oscillation comes from a loop between two elements.** x drives the change of v, and v drives the change of x, so each one keeps feeding the other. >> This is the simplest picture of a vibrating dynamical system. >> It is the same story as a mass on a spring, where the position drives the spring's push and the motion carries the mass past equilibrium.

9|**Almost everything can be treated as a harmonic oscillator, if one is brave enough.** Vibrations appear in the air of the vocal tract, in the vocal folds, in the economy, in music and even in the matter everything is made of. >> Recognizing one shared pattern in such different places is the creative leap the course keeps asking for.
10|**A slice of air in the vocal tract is also an oscillator.** Where the bits of air are squeezed very close together, the pressure is high, and where they are spread far apart, the pressure is low. >> As a sound passes through, the air at each spot keeps swinging between being squeezed together and being spread apart. >> So the air in the mouth is doing exactly what x does in the x and v system.
11|**There is a cosine in your mouth.** The pressure of the air inside the vocal tract rises and falls over time in the same smooth wave that the x and v system produces. >> That wave is the shape of a cosine, which is the signature of a harmonic oscillator. >> But in each vowel, the air does not vibrate at just one frequency, because several of these waves are present at once.
12|**Vowels differ in the shape of the vocal tract, and two dimensions describe them.** One dimension is **height**, how high the tongue is, which ranges from high to low. The other is **backness**, how far forward the tongue is, which ranges from front through central to back. >> Dean is high and front, while Dune is high and back, with the lips rounded as well. >> Dan is low and front, Don is low and back, and Done sits in the central region.

15|**Two different things vibrate during speech.** One is the air in the vocal tract, whose pressure swings like a cosine, and the other is the pair of vocal folds in the larynx. >> The vocal folds open and close very quickly, and their vibration is what gives voicing its buzz. >> So speech involves one vibrating system driving another, with the vocal folds setting the air in the tract into vibration.
16|**The economy also behaves like a harmonic oscillator.** The business cycle swings through peaks and troughs, from recession to recovery and back again, around an overall trend of growth. >> Each swing overshoots the trend in one direction and then comes back and overshoots in the other, just as x does. >> So the same mathematics that describes air in the mouth also describes booms and busts.

18|**Music is all about harmonic oscillators.** Every note comes from something vibrating, such as a string, a column of air in a wind instrument or the skin of a drum. >> The written score is a set of instructions for which vibrations to produce and when.
19|**Even matter is made of oscillators.** Atoms contain electrons that behave like vibrations, some at low frequencies and some at high frequencies, and physicists describe them with a version of the harmonic oscillator. >> So the people studying the air in the mouth and the people studying atoms are using the same basic idea. >> That is the strongest sense in which almost everything is a harmonic oscillator.
20|**The x and v system can also be run in Python.** A short program starts x and v at some values and repeatedly applies the two rules, a little at a time, to move both forward through time. >> Plotting x over time from such a program produces the same up and down wave as the reasoning in words. >> Programming the system is a way of checking the reasoning, since the computer only follows the two rules and nothing else.
