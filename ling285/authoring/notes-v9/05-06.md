## p3
- **A harmonic oscillator is built from only two quantities, x and v.** The quantity x is how far something is displaced from its resting point, and v is its **velocity**, how fast and in which direction x is moving. The whole system is about **how x and v change each other over time**.
  - A dot over a variable means its change over time, in **Isaac Newton's notation**. A positive ẋ means x is rising, a negative ẋ means x is falling, and the size of ẋ says how fast. The same holds for v̇.
- **The first rule is that ẋ equals v, so v steers x.** Whatever sign v has is the direction x moves. **The second rule is that v̇ equals minus x**, and the **minus sign** is the heart of the system. The quantity v is always pushed in the direction opposite to wherever x currently is.

## p4
- **If v equals negative 5, then ẋ is negative 5, so x will go down.** The value of x does not matter for this. Only the sign of v decides whether x rises or falls.
- **If x equals negative 5, then v̇ is minus negative 5, which is positive 5, so v will go up.** The two minus signs cancel. This is the restoring behavior in action, since an upward v then lifts x back toward zero.
  - The positive cases read the same way. A v of positive 5 makes x rise, and an x of positive 5 makes v fall. These are statements about the direction of change, so a velocity that is going up may still be negative, moving from a large negative value toward zero.

## p5
- **The two rules treat the sign differently.** The rule ẋ equals v keeps the sign, so positive velocity makes position rise. The rule v̇ equals minus x reverses the sign, so positive position makes velocity fall.
- **Whenever x moves away from zero, the second rule turns v around, and the first rule then carries x back toward zero.** This **restoring relationship** keeps the system swinging back and forth instead of drifting off. Keeping each quantity separate from its rate of change makes the motion understandable.

## p6
- **Start with x at its highest positive value and v at zero.** Because x is positive, v drops below zero, so x starts to decrease, slowly at first. As v gets more negative, x falls faster. When x reaches zero, v̇ is zero, v is at its most negative, and x crosses zero at its fastest.
  - Once x is below zero, v̇ turns positive and v rises back toward zero while x keeps falling more slowly. When v returns to zero, x is at its lowest point and turns around. With x negative, v becomes positive, x climbs, v peaks as x crosses zero, and x returns to its maximum as v comes back to zero. Then the whole cycle repeats.
- **Computing the system at a series of separate moments gives points that connect into two smooth waves.** The x wave starts at the top, and the v wave starts at zero and dips negative first. The two waves have the same shape but are shifted by a quarter of a cycle. The value v is at its extremes when x crosses zero, and v is zero when x is at its extremes.

## p7
- **A dynamical system is a set of variables together with rules for how they change over time, and the x-v system is an oscillation or vibration dynamical system.** Oscillation and vibration both mean a quantity repeatedly swinging back and forth around a middle value.
- **The relationship is a closed loop of influence.** One arrow runs from v to x for ẋ equals v, and another runs from x back to v for v̇ equals minus x. A change in v changes x, which changes v again, so once the system is set in motion it keeps cycling with nothing outside pushing it. Each cycle reaches the same height and depth and takes the same time.

## p9
- **The same structure of ẋ equals v and v̇ equals minus x shows up in the air of the vocal tract, the vocal folds, economies, music and matter itself.** Calling a system a harmonic oscillator means finding what plays the role of x, a displacement from rest, and what plays the role of v, its rate of change. It is a simplification, but it captures the basic back-and-forth pattern they all share.

## p10
- **A thin slice of air in the vocal tract contains many particles, and its pressure depends on how close together they are.** **High pressure** means the particles are very close together, and **low pressure** means they are very far apart. The particles stay the same in number and size. Only the spacing between them changes.
- **Compression squeezes the particles closer than their equilibrium spacing, and rarefaction spreads them farther apart.** One pair of neighbors sits at 100 percent of its equilibrium distance at equilibrium, about 56 percent at the strongest compression, and about 141 percent at the strongest rarefaction. The slice cycles between these states, with real speech moving far less than these exaggerated values.
- **The slice behaves like a harmonic oscillator.** The spacing relative to equilibrium plays the role of x. Squeezed air pushes itself apart and stretched air pulls itself back together, so any departure is pushed back toward equilibrium, the same restoring relationship as v̇ equals minus x.

## p11
- **The slice's motion follows a cosine, so x equals the cosine of the phase.** Here x is the **normalized expansion**, scaled so plus 1 is the largest stretch, maximum rarefaction, 0 is **equilibrium**, and minus 1 is the largest squeeze, maximum compression.
- **The phase is the position within a cycle, and each 2π is one full cycle**, so 0 to 6π covers three cycles. At phase 0 the cosine is plus 1, at π over 2 it is 0, at π it is minus 1, at 3π over 2 it is 0 again, and at 2π it is back to plus 1. Values along the way, such as plus 0.74, minus 0.54, plus 0.31 or minus 0.21, say by their sign whether the slice is rarefied or compressed.
- **This is the same wave the x-v system produces when x starts at its maximum and v at zero.** The vibrating air in the vocal tract follows the harmonic oscillator relationship.

## p12
- **Vowels differ in where the tongue sits inside the vocal tract**, as seen in MRI side views with the speaker facing left. Vowels are organized by **height**, **high** or **low**, and by **frontness**, **front**, **back** or **central**.
  - **"Dean"** is high and front. **"Dune"** is high and back. **"Dan"** is low and front. **"Don"** is low and back. **"Done"** is central, with the tongue resting in the middle of the mouth.
- **Each tongue position gives the air a different shape, and a differently shaped column of air vibrates differently.** That difference in vibration is what makes one vowel sound different from another.

## p13
- **A vowel is a sum of vibrations at several frequencies at once, so it can be modeled as harmonic oscillators added together.** **Frequency** is cycles per second in **hertz**, and a vibration at f hertz is the cosine of 2π times f times t.
- **The vowel /i/ in "dean" is modeled as 1 Hz plus 4 Hz, the cosine of 2πt plus the cosine of 8πt**, a slow wave with fast ripples on top. **The vowel /a/ in "Don" is modeled as 2 Hz plus 3 Hz, the cosine of 4πt plus the cosine of 6πt**, whose close frequencies give a different pattern of tall and short swings.
  - These frequencies are example values, not measured ones, and the components have **equal amplitudes**, so each sum ranges between plus 2 and minus 2 and reaches plus 2 at 0, 1, 2 and 3 seconds. In between, the components partly cancel or reinforce, so /i/ can be at minus 1.21 while /a/ is at plus 0.58.
- **Positive x still means rarefaction and negative x still means compression.** The two vowels squeeze and stretch the slice of air in two different time patterns, and those patterns distinguish them.

## p14
- **Praat is a free program for recording and analyzing speech.** It shows the **waveform**, air pressure changing over time, and breaks a sound into its frequencies. Recording "dean" and "Don" shows each vowel's repeating waveform made of several frequencies added together, the same idea as the two-frequency models.

## p15
- **Two things vibrate during speech, the air in the vocal tract and the vocal folds.** The **vocal folds** are two folds of tissue in the **larynx**. Air from the lungs pushes them apart, the pressure drops as air rushes through, and they come back together, many times per second.
  - Each opening lets through a puff of air, so the folds chop the airflow into rapid puffs. Those puffs set the vocal tract air vibrating, and the tongue's shaping of the tract sets which frequencies it vibrates at.
- **A vowel is one oscillator driving another.** The folds are moving tissue, and the air is changing compression and rarefaction, so an explanation of speech must account for both.

## p16
- **The business cycle, the repeated rise and fall of economic activity, is a harmonic oscillator.** It plots **time** against the **level of real output**, goods and services adjusted for price changes. Output moves from a **peak** through a **recession** to a **trough**, then through a **recovery** to the next peak.
- **The swings happen around a rising trend line**, so each later peak can be higher than the one before. The distance of output above or below the trend plays the role of x. Above the trend output falls back, and below the trend it rises back, the same pattern as ẋ equals v and v̇ equals minus x.

## p18
- **Music is built from harmonic oscillators.** Every note is a vibration at a particular frequency, whether from a plucked string, a column of air or a reed, and **pitch** corresponds to frequency. When several notes sound together their vibrations add, the way the 1 Hz and 4 Hz cosines add to form /i/.

## p19
- **At the scale of atoms, the harmonic oscillator appears as the quantum harmonic oscillator, whose energy is quantized**, meaning it can only take certain specific levels. Matter is made of atoms with different numbers of electrons, some vibrating at low frequency and some at high frequency, and electrons are **wave modes** rather than tiny balls on tracks.
- **Magnesium shows two ways of drawing an atom.** It has 12 **electrons** and 12 **protons**, with ground-state configuration 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s². The older **Bohr-style shell** drawing puts electrons on circular paths in shells of 2, 8 and 2. The **quantum orbital** description adds the **electron density** of each occupied orbital, 1s², 2s², 2p⁶ and 3s², into a total density, with darker shading where an electron is more likely to be found.
  - Orbitals are **quantized wave modes**, but **magnesium is not a harmonic-oscillator atom**. Real atoms follow related but different rules.

## p20
- **The x-v system can be computed step by step in Python.** Start with x at 1 and v at 0, and at each small step of size dt set ẋ equal to v and v̇ equal to minus x. The new x is the old x plus ẋ times dt, and the new v is the old v plus v̇ times dt. Smaller steps follow the true smooth waves more closely.
- **The program uses numpy arrays, dt of 0.01 and 1000 steps, loops the two update lines, and plots x and v against t.** The output reproduces the oscillator. The value x traces a cosine from the top, and v starts at zero, drops negative first, and traces the same shape shifted by a quarter cycle.
