## p2
- Everything in modern AI grows from **one very simple idea**, the **neuron**, which takes information from other neurons through an **inner product**. A **neural network** repeats that operation layer after layer to make a decision, but a network that makes one **static decision** cannot capture cognition that **unfolds in time**.
- **Language depends on long-distance relationships between words.** In "the dog that my parents, who I love very much, who live in a state far, far away," the next word is **barks**, because it goes with the singular **dog** no matter how many words come in between.
  - Speakers absorb this rule without being taught, and an LLM learns it the same way, from data, which is why it needs so many layers.
- **An agentic system is a team of LLMs that talk to each other in English.** An outline LLM plans, a writing LLM writes, a critic LLM checks, and a revision LLM loops with the critic, sometimes under a **controller** agent.
  - The agents are also **multilingual in computer languages**, such as Python and database languages, because they were trained to predict whole programs too.
  - Agents share a **common memory**, which is a big source of their power and also of the danger, because it lets them coordinate in ways nobody planned.
- **Nobody fully understands what all the weights are doing**, because the designs evolved by tinkering over about 75 years, much as dogs were bred into today's breeds.

## p3
- **Predicting the next word is so difficult that an LLM needs many layers, and in each layer it needs many parallel heads.** A head is a separate expert, and in each layer the knowledge from all the heads is brought together to produce **improved vectors**.
  - This is a question for the exam. **Layers go one after another from the input to the output, and heads sit side by side within each layer.**
- **A classroom comparison makes heads and layers clear.** Imagine trying to improve oneself by visiting a series of classes, one after another.
  - In each class, every student gives an independent opinion, and each student is like a **head**.
  - The opinions are **combined** into one improved version, which is what a layer does with its heads, and then the next class is visited. **The classes are the layers.**
  - In plain terms, layers give an LLM its depth, one round of improvement after another, and heads give it width, many experts consulted at once within each round.
- **Each head is a set of weights**, and large systems have **a hundred or more layers** with on the order of **a hundred heads** in each. When the system predicts the wrong word, **every head in every layer** adjusts its weights through backpropagation.

## p4
- **A dynamical system is a rule that is fed back to itself.** Numbers go in, the rule acts on them, and the output is fed back in as the next input, again and again.
  - In the **harmonic oscillator**, x and v **take turns**, the new x computed from the old v and the new v from the new x, and step by step the rule draws a **cosine**.
  - Fields as far apart as finance use the same idea with different rules.
- **Jordan's network was a marriage of neural networks and dynamical systems.** Its **output is copied back into its state units**, and **plan units** and **state units** feed the hidden layer, with the same weights used at every step. After more than **thirty years of tinkering**, this idea became the **LLM**.
- **In an LLM, the layer is the rule**, and **depth plays the role of time**. Each layer applies its weights to every vector, and following one number up through the layers traces out a curve, much like the oscillator's curve, though not an exact cosine.
  - The rule in each layer is the weights of its heads, learned through backpropagation by predicting the next word.
  - The goal is to understand this general structure, not to memorize formulas.

## p5
- An LLM is given **15 vectors** and asked to predict the **16th**, and the vectors can come from anywhere, because the machine only ever sees lists of numbers.
  - They can be **15 words** of a sentence, **15 frames of a spectrogram**, **15 patches of an image** read row by row, or **15 amino acids** in a protein, such as the B chain of insulin.
- **The same design and the same prediction game work for language, speech, vision and proteins.**

## p6
- Speakers know a small system of **only a few dozen sounds** that decides how vowels and consonants can follow each other, which makes it a good test case for a small LLM. **A simple English syllable is a consonant, then a vowel, then a consonant**, and only certain **clusters** are allowed, so **sblit**, **lfat** and **desb** are impossible.
- **Every language has its own rules for sequences.** Mandarin allows only nasal sounds at the end of a syllable, Cantonese also allows a final p, Russian and Polish allow clusters like zb or dl at the start of a word, and Spanish speakers add an e before a word like stadium.

## p7
- Guessing the next letter of an English word reveals this sound knowledge directly. **After a vowel, almost anything can follow**, but **at the start of a word the choices narrow sharply**, so after **sp** only l, r or a vowel can come, and after **skr** only a vowel.
- **Guessing the next letter is predicting the 16th vector.** A GPT that only plays this game learns English sounds and spelling, with a **flat** distribution after a vowel and a **sharply peaked** one after skr.

## p8
- An LLM that learns English syllables can be built by **describing it in English** to an agentic system, specifying **five layers**, **four heads in each layer**, and **input vectors 64 numbers long**, then training it on **1000 English words** to **predict the next letter** and showing ten generated words after each step.
  - The system built a small **character-level transformer** with **attention heads**, where **attention** means a head consults the rest of the sequence to work out which preceding items matter, just as predicting barks requires attending to dog.
  - After training, the generated words were **still mostly gibberish**, and training on **10,000 words** for many more steps was the suggested next change.
- **Software 1.0** is writing programs, **Software 2.0** is letting a network's **weights** become the program, and **Software 3.0** is using an agentic system to build and run a program from plain English, which is called **vibe coding**.
- The first attempt at this kind of problem was **Claude Shannon's 1948 paper, "A Mathematical Theory of Communication,"** which generated letter sequences closer and closer to English by hand and founded **information theory**, which remains important for LLMs today.
