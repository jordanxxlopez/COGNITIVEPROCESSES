## p2
- A **neural network** can act as a very simple **speech recognizer**. It takes the **two formants, F1 and F2**, of a vowel and decides which of **five words** it heard, **done, dune, dean, Don or Dan**. Networks like this are the **atoms** that modern **large language models** are built from.
- The network has **2 input neurons**, **three hidden layers** with **3, 2 and 4 neurons**, and **5 output neurons**, and every neuron connects to every neuron in the next layer.
  - The **input neurons** just take numbers in from the environment, such as one speaker's F1 of 400 and F2 of 1900.
  - **The knowledge of the network is in its weights.** Each connection has a weight, a positive or negative number, and the whole network is nothing more than a machine for processing numbers with those weights.

## p3
- **Forward propagation** carries one input through the network to a decision. The example input is **F1 of 320 and F2 of 2000**, which has the pattern of **dean**.
  - Each neuron computes an **inner product**. For the top neuron of the first hidden layer, the weight on F1 is multiplied by 320, the weight on F2 is multiplied by 2000, and the two products are added.
  - The result of that inner product, with no other change, is called the neuron's **linear activation**, and here it comes out slightly below zero.
  - The activations of each layer become the inputs to the next layer, which does more inner products, until the output layer.
- At the end, the output numbers are turned into a **probability distribution**, numbers between zero and one that **add up to one** and stand for the network's **decision**. The operation that does this is called **softmax**, and all that matters about it is that a bigger number gives a bigger probability.
  - Here the network gives **done about 56 percent**, so it says, in effect, **"I heard done,"** even though the input was dean.
- **Knowledge has been reduced to positive and negative numbers, and inference has been reduced to multiplying and adding.** The systems people chat with every day make their decisions with nothing but these elementary operations, which are close to what the chemistry inside real neurons does.

## p4
- Because every step is an inner product, the effect of raising one number can be worked out without any arithmetic. Take the top neuron of the last hidden layer, with an activation of about 1.57, and its weight of positive 0.1 into the **done** output.
  - If that **activation goes up**, its contribution to the done output goes up, so the **probability of done increases**.
  - If that **weight goes up**, from 0.1 to 0.2, the contribution also grows, so the **probability of done increases** again.
  - **A positive number times a positive number is positive**, so raising either one makes that neuron push the output up more.
- A good answer names the direction, up or down, and then the reason, never the multiplication.
  - In plain terms, each neuron votes for each output through its weight, and a positive vote from an active neuron always helps that output.

## p6
- **Every network starts out stupid**, because its weights start out **random**, and it becomes smarter only by being told the right answer.
  - The **desired output** for a recording of dean is **0, 0, 1, 0, 0**, a 100 percent chance it was dean, which is called a **one-hot encoding**.
  - The gap between the **computed output** and the desired output is the network's **error**.
- **Backpropagation** changes the weights so that the same input leads to a better decision. **Learning happens gradually, one step at a time**, just as with stochastic gradient descent, and the **learning rate** sets how big each step is.
  - The weights are updated **starting from the end**, first the weights into the output, then into the third hidden layer, then the second, then the first, while the forward activations stay as they were.
  - This is why AI needs huge **data centers**, since a modern chatbot can have **trillions of weights** that must be nudged over and over for months.

## p7
- For the **last weight**, the 0.1 that links the top neuron of the last hidden layer to **done**, the effect is easy to see. Raising it would raise the probability of done, but the desired probability of done is **zero**, so raising it would make the error **worse**, and it should be **lowered**.
- For the **first weight**, the 0.005 near the input, the effect is very hard to see. Changing it changes the first hidden layer, which changes the second and the third, which finally change the output.
  - **Its effect on the decision is mediated by all the intermediate calculations**, so just by looking at it, there is no way to tell whether it should go up or down. Backpropagation still works it out.

## p8
- **The last weights directly determine the output probabilities**, so the error can be used right away to improve them. **The first weights affect their activations, which affect two more layers of activations**, and their effect on the error **includes the effect of the last weights**, which are downstream of them.
- So **improving the last weights is the easy problem**, and improving the first weights is a much bigger problem that already contains the easy one. Solving small problems first so they can be used for bigger ones is **dynamic programming**, developed by **Bellman** and **Viterbi**.
  - **Backpropagation is stochastic gradient descent plus dynamic programming.** Stochastic gradient descent changes the numbers gradually, and dynamic programming supplies the **back** in backpropagation.
  - Backpropagation was worked out in the **mid-1980s**, and a 2024 Nobel Prize recognized it, because there would be no AI without it.
  - The short version of the exam answer is that the last weight affects the decision in one simple step, while the first weight's effect goes through many layers and depends on the later weights, so the easy problem is solved first and reused for the harder ones.

## p9
- Training needs two tables of numbers, called **matrices**, and each row pairs **one recording** with **the vowel it really was**.
  - The **input matrix** has two columns, F1 and F2, so with about 110 recordings it is 110 by 2.
  - The **output matrix** has five columns, one for each word, with a **1 marking the true vowel**, so it is 110 by 5.

## p10

  - The **import** lines bring in the torch library, which does all the background work, and the **load** lines read the formant matrix as X and the true-vowel matrix as Y.

  - In plain terms, the program describes the network, shows it the data, measures how wrong it is, works backward to find who is to blame, and adjusts the weights a little.

## p11
- **Lord Edgar Adrian** won the **Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1932** for discovering that **neurons are nearly digital**, roughly **on or off**. The network's neurons so far are not like that, because their activations can be any real numbers.
- The **sigmoid** is an **activation function** that takes the inner product and squashes it.
  - A **large negative** inner product, such as minus 4, minus 10 or minus 100, gives about **0**, so the neuron is **off**.
  - A **large positive** inner product, such as 4 or 100, gives about **1**, so the neuron is **on**.
  - Only inner products **near zero** get values in between, so the sigmoid **digitizes** the neuron.
- The sigmoid also matches something about **perception**. **Some things that are very different are perceived as the same, and some things that are very similar are perceived as different.**
  - A friend is the same person with a hat or without one, just as the sigmoid gives minus 1000 and minus 20 the same value.
  - USC and UCLA are alike in many ways yet perceived as completely different, just as two inputs just below and just above zero land on opposite sides of the sigmoid's jump.
  - In plain terms, the flat parts of the curve lump different things together, and the steep middle splits nearly identical things apart.

## p12
- Adding the sigmoid to each hidden neuron changes every number downstream. The first hidden neuron, slightly below zero, becomes about **0.4**, and the second, about 6.6, becomes about **1**, and each change recalculates the later layers.
- With the same random weights and the sigmoid on all nine hidden neurons, the network spreads its probability more evenly and gives its highest probability to **Dan**, so it is **still wrong** about dean. Only training with backpropagation can give it good weights.

## p13
- The program changes in only one way. **nn.Sigmoid** is inserted after each **hidden** linear layer, which means "apply the sigmoid," or make that layer digital.
  - The **last layer has no sigmoid**, because its outputs go straight into the loss calculation, which turns them into probabilities.
- Training both versions tests whether the sigmoid makes the vowel recognizer better or worse, and other functions such as **ReLU** can be swapped in too. **Many of the most basic ideas in AI come from how real brains work.**
