2|A **dynamic system** is a system that changes over time because its parts affect each other. The idea is not limited to springs. Economists use it to describe how a whole economy rises and falls. >> **Woodford’s theory**, called **Dynamic Stochastic General Equilibrium** or **DSGE**, treats the economy this way. >> The name means the economy changes over time, gets hit by random shocks and has many linked markets that move toward balance together. || Two quantities matter here, **unemployment** and **inflation**. >> Without stabilization, both swing up and down in large, sharp jumps. >> With stabilization, the swings become smaller and smoother. || **Stabilization** means making the swings smaller. >> The economy still changes over time. >> The goal is to avoid harmful extremes, like very high unemployment or inflation that runs out of control. || A spring and an economy share one basic question. What causes the swings, and what can calm them down? >> In a spring, inertia and the restoring force keep the mass moving back and forth. >> In an economy, shocks and the way its parts respond keep the numbers moving up and down.

9|The behavior of these systems comes down to a short list of **principles of mass–spring systems**. || The first two are about the whole system. >> **More mass lowers the frequency, and more stiffness raises it.** A heavy load is slow to turn around, and a stiff spring pulls back hard. >> **A system with n masses has n modes**, and each mode is faster than the one before it. || The next three say that **location matters**. >> Added mass lowers a mode’s frequency only as much as that mass actually moves in the mode. >> Added stiffness raises a mode’s frequency only as much as that spring actually stretches or squeezes. >> Movement and stretching peak in different places. Where parts move the most, they move together, so nothing stretches. Where stretching is greatest, the middle point hardly moves. || So the same change can matter a lot in one mode and very little in another. >> A spot that swings widely in a mode feels added mass strongly. >> A spot that stays still in a mode barely feels it. || The last two are about the ends of the system. >> When **both ends are fixed**, the harmonics are whole-number multiples of the lowest frequency. >> When **one end is fixed and one is free**, the harmonics are **odd multiples** of the lowest frequency.

11|This **pop quiz** asks what happens when mass or stiffness is added at one spot on a string, and how that change affects **Mode 4** and **Mode 5**. || The two modes use that spot in different ways. >> In **Mode 4**, the spot swings strongly. Its neighbors swing with it, so the string there hardly stretches. >> In **Mode 5**, the spot barely moves. Its neighbors move differently, so the string there stretches a lot. || **Adding mass** works through movement. >> In Mode 4, the extra mass gets carried through a big swing, so the mode slows down. >> In Mode 5, the spot hardly moves, so the extra mass has almost no effect. >> Mass only lowers the frequency when the mass is moving. || **Adding stiffness** works through stretching. >> In Mode 5, the string at the spot stretches a lot, so a stiffer spring there makes the mode faster. >> In Mode 4, the string at the spot barely stretches, so the extra stiffness has little effect. || Adding **both** at once **lowers Mode 4 and raises Mode 5**. >> The spot is the same in both modes. >> What differs is its job. In one mode it mostly swings, and in the other it mostly stretches.

15|The air in the vocal tract can be pictured as a chain of masses joined by springs. >> Moving air has **inertia**, so it tends to keep moving. That is the mass. >> Squeezed air pushes back. That is the spring. >> The air moves **back and forth** along the tube, around its usual place. It does not stream steadily out of the mouth. || The tube has one fixed end and one free end. >> The **glottis** is the fixed end, where the air has little room to move. >> The open **lips** are the free end, where the air moves most easily. || The two slowest patterns of this tube match the first two formants. >> **Mode 1 is like F1**, the lowest resonance. >> **Mode 2 is like F2**, with one more back-and-forth reversal inside the tube. >> In a perfectly even tube, Mode 2 runs **three times** as fast as Mode 1. A different tube shape changes that. || Each mode is one pattern of motion shared by the whole column of air. >> In some places, neighboring bits of air move together. >> In other places, the gaps between them keep opening and closing. || Three vowels narrow the tube at three places. >> **/a/** narrows it about **one-third** of the way up from the glottis. >> **/i/** narrows it about **two-thirds** of the way up. >> **/u/** narrows it at the **lips**. >> Mass matters where a mode moves, and springiness matters where it squeezes. So one narrowing can push F1 and F2 in different directions.
16|Vowels are usually described by **tongue height** and by whether the tongue is **front or back**. Each vowel also narrows the airway at one particular place. || The two high vowels narrow the airway in different places. >> **Dean** is **high and front**. It narrows about **two-thirds** of the way from the glottis to the lips. >> **Dune** is **high and back**. It narrows at the **lips**. >> They are both high, but their tract shapes are different. || The two low vowels are at the bottom. >> **Dan** is **low and front**. >> **Don** is **low and back**. It narrows about **one-third** of the way from the glottis. || **Done** is in the **center**, with **no constriction**. >> It is the open shape that the other vowels are compared with. || Each label describes a different feature. >> High or low tells how high the tongue is. >> Front or back tells where the tongue sits in the mouth. >> The fraction tells how far along the air path the narrowing is. || The place of narrowing links the mouth to the sound. >> Narrowing the airway changes how the air behaves at that spot. >> Each formant then shifts according to how its mode moves and squeezes the air there.

19|For **/u/**, the lips are rounded and narrowed. This is the **free end** of the air column, and narrowing it adds more **effective mass**, which means more air has to be moved back and forth at the lips. || Both of the lowest modes move strongly at the lips. >> In **Mode 1**, **motion dominates** at the lips. >> In **Mode 2**, **motion dominates** at the lips too. || A heavier moving load is slower to speed up and slower to turn around. >> Each cycle takes longer. >> Both modes slow down, so **F1 goes down and F2 goes down**. || The two modes are shaped differently inside the tube. >> But they both swing strongly at the lips, so the extra load slows both of them. >> F1 is still the lower formant, and F2 is still the higher one. || Both formants fall because the lips play the same role in both modes. >> In both, the air at the lips mostly moves. >> Narrowing in other places can work differently, so this is not true of every narrowing.
20|For **/i/**, the tongue narrows the airway near the front, about **two-thirds** of the way from the glottis to the lips. It is one spot, but that spot plays a different part in each mode. || In **Mode 1**, **mass motion dominates** at that spot. >> The air there swings a lot in this mode. >> Narrowing it adds moving mass, and moving mass is slow to turn around. >> The mode slows down, so **F1 goes down**. || In **Mode 2**, **springiness dominates** at that same spot. >> The air there is squeezed and stretched a lot in this mode. >> Narrowing it makes the air push back harder. >> The mode speeds up, so **F2 goes up**. || The spot stays in the same place. What changes is its job in each mode. >> Where the air mostly swings, added mass matters most. >> Where the air mostly squeezes, added springiness matters most. || So **/i/** has a **lower F1 and a higher F2** than the even tube. >> The two effects do not cancel each other out. >> Each mode follows the effect that is stronger at that spot in its own pattern.
21|For **/a/**, the narrowing is farther back, about **one-third** of the way up from the glottis. At this spot, the two modes switch the roles they had for /i/. || In **Mode 1**, **springiness dominates** at this spot. >> The air here is squeezed and stretched a lot in this mode. >> Narrowing it makes the air push back harder, so the mode speeds up and **F1 goes up**. || In **Mode 2**, **mass motion dominates** at this spot. >> The air here swings a lot in this mode. >> Narrowing it adds moving mass, so the mode slows down and **F2 goes down**. || One spot can have two different jobs. >> In one job, the spot barely moves, but the air on each side crowds in and spreads out. That is squeezing. >> In the other job, the spot moves a lot, but the gaps around it stay about the same. That is swinging. >> At the one-third point, Mode 1 gives the spot a squeezing job, and Mode 2 gives it a swinging job. || So **/a/** has a **higher F1 and a lower F2**, the reverse of /i/. >> Moving the narrowing changes which job each mode gives it. >> Mass and stiffness still work the same way they always do.

23|Every spot in a vibrating column of air can be described in two ways, by its **movement** and by its **compression**. >> **Movement** is how far a bit of air travels. >> **Compression** is how the gaps between neighboring bits change. When the gaps shrink, the air is **compressed**. When they grow, the air is **thinned out**, which is called **rarefaction**. || These are two different things. >> Neighbors can travel a long way together while their gaps stay the same. >> Neighbors can also crowd together and spread apart around a bit of air that barely moves. || Movement and compression act like a mass and a spring. >> Crowded air pushes back toward its normal spacing. That is the spring. >> Moving air keeps going because of inertia. That is the mass. >> Together, they keep the air moving back and forth around its usual place. || Each higher mode fits more alternating stretches of movement and compression into the same tube. >> Where movement is large, there is a lot of moving mass. >> Where the gaps change a lot, there is strong push-back. >> These places are different in each mode. || This tells where a narrowing will matter. >> Added mass matters most where the air moves a lot. >> Added stiffness matters most where the air is squeezed a lot. >> Each mode is a separate resonance of the same air, not a separate stream of air.
