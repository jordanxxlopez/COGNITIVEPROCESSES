8|Comparing the **last** weights with the **first** weights reveals why training runs backward. || The **last weights, W5**, set the output probabilities directly. >> The error depends very directly on them. >> The gap between computed and desired outputs points straight to better W5 values. || The **first weights, W2**, sit far from the error. >> W2 shapes the first hidden activations. >> Those pass through two more layers and other weights before any output appears. >> W2’s share of the blame is spread across every path that follows it. >> That share **includes** the effect of W5, since W5 lies downstream. || **W5 is the easy problem**, and W2 builds on it. >> Solving small problems first and reusing their answers for larger, dependent problems is **dynamic programming**. >> **Backpropagation is Bellman–Viterbi dynamic programming.** >> It computes the output-side effects once and reuses them for each earlier layer. || **Backpropagation = SGD + dynamic programming.** >> **SGD**, stochastic gradient descent, adjusts the weights. >> Backpropagation supplies the gradients SGD needs, efficiently, through dynamic programming.

11|The **sigmoid activation function**, **σ(z) = 1 / (1 + exp(−z))**, turns an inner product z into a value between 0 and 1. || Large inputs land near the limits. >> A **large positive** inner product gives a sigmoid near **1**. >> A **large negative** inner product gives a sigmoid near **0**. >> **σ(−4) = 0.0180**, and **σ(4) = 0.9820**. || The curve is an S. >> The **linear activation**, the weighted sum, runs along the horizontal axis, and the **sigmoid activation** runs up the vertical axis. >> It climbs steeply through the middle and levels off at both ends. >> On the flat ends, large input changes produce only small output changes. || In the formula, a large positive z shrinks exp(−z) toward zero, pushing the output toward 1. >> A large negative z makes exp(−z) enormous, pushing the output toward 0. || **Lord Edgar Adrian**, **Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, 1932**, provides the biological motivation. >> **Neurons are nearly digital**, roughly on or off. >> His work concerned firing rates, and the sigmoid models that graded, nearly on-off behavior. || **Animal perception** follows the same pattern. >> Some similar things are perceived as different, and some very different things are perceived as the same. >> Inputs on either side of the steep middle give very different outputs, while inputs on a flat end give nearly identical ones.
