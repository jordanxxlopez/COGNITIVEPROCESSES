6|Every point in an air column has a **movement** and a **compression**. >> **Movement** is the distance an air element travels. >> **Compression** is the change in spacing between neighbors. >> Neighbors can travel together with steady gaps, and gaps can close and open strongly around a nearly still point. || Each property has its own reading. >> One circle followed through a cycle gives movement. >> The gaps between neighboring circles give compression, squeezed in some regions and spread in others. || Together they act as spring and mass. >> Changing compression provides a **spring-like restoring effect**, as crowded air pushes back toward normal spacing. >> **Inertia** keeps the air moving as that push changes. || The tube’s ends set the layout. >> The **closed end** holds movement back. >> The **open end** lets the air move. >> Each higher mode places its alternating regions at its own positions in the tube. || The moving **Schwa** reference and the **F1, F2 and F3** rows are resonances of one air system. >> A constriction can act on moving mass in one mode and on compression in another. >> Its effect depends on the mode and on where it sits.
7|The **pop quiz** labels six spots along the air column, **A** to **F**, and asks how each one behaves. >> Each spot is followed through a full cycle. >> Its air element’s travel gives its movement. >> The spacing changes around it give its compression. >> A full cycle is what reveals the spot’s usual behavior. || At a spot with strong **movement**, added mass is what counts. >> More **effective mass** is slower to speed up and reverse. >> The cycle lengthens, and the resonance **falls**. || At a spot with strong **compression change**, added stiffness is what counts. >> More **stiffness** pushes the air back toward its spacing more strongly. >> The cycle shortens, and the resonance **rises**. || **Modes 1, 2 and 3** lay these regions out differently. >> A spot that moves strongly in one mode can be squeezed strongly in another. >> A prediction needs both the position and the mode.

16|The vocal tract is a **filter**, a **fixed–free** tube closed at one end and open at the other, and it selects **formants**. >> A formant **encourages the closest harmonics to be audible**. >> **Harmonics far from that formant are muted.** >> The source supplies the harmonics, the filter decides which survive, and the vowel shape is the filter. || Each vowel sets its formants in its own places. >> **Schwa** has well-separated first and second resonances, near **500** and **1500 Hz**. >> **/u/** lowers both, near **300** and **1000 Hz**, and adds a third near **2000 Hz**. >> **/i/ or /ɪ/** pairs a low F1 near **300 Hz** with a high F2 near **2200 Hz**. >> **/a/** places its first two targets side by side at **800** and **900 Hz**. || Moving the tongue and lips moves these favored regions. >> Each target lifts the harmonics closest to it. >> Harmonics between targets stay weak. || A formant target belongs to the tract, and a harmonic line belongs to the source. >> A target can sit between two lines and strengthen both neighbors. >> Filtering adjusts the heights of the lines the source supplies.
