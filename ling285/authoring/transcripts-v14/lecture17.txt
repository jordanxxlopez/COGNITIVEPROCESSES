2|The vowel network stands for neural networks from **1943 to 1986**. >> Two formants pass through three weighted hidden layers to five word outputs. >> The weights are the same illustrative signed weights as before. || **McCulloch and Pitts (1943)** begin the period. >> **“A Logical Calculus of the Ideas Immanent in Nervous Activity”** starts from the **“all-or-none” character of nervous activity**. >> It treats neural events with propositional logic. || The **Parallel Distributed Processing** volumes of **1986** end it. >> They popularized trainable networks of many simple connected units. || **Given some static vector of formants, the NN could make a decision about a few possible categories.** >> A static vector is one fixed list of numbers from one moment. >> Here it is F1 and F2, and the decision is one of five words. || **Human cognition is far more complex.** >> Speech and sentences are sequences, where what comes next depends on what came before. >> A network that maps one fixed vector to a few labels cannot track earlier inputs.
3|In **1985, Michael Jordan developed recurrent neural networks** for **speech production**. >> That work led toward **LLMs** and then **agentic AI**. >> The photo is Michael Jordan. || The network has four kinds of units. >> **Plan units** say which sequence to produce. >> **Hidden units** combine, and **output units** respond. >> Outputs loop back into **state units**, which also feed back to themselves. || The loops make it **recurrent**. >> State units hold a trace of the latest outputs. >> Each new output depends on the plan and on what the network just produced. || **Performance on /istrstry/** plots eight features over 32 time steps. >> The rows are **Voice, Place, Sonorant, Sibilant, Nasal, Height, Back and Round**. >> Sibilant peaks at the s sounds, while Nasal and Height stay flat. || **Round** rises early and stays high. >> A feature spreads across several sounds instead of switching for each one. >> Feeding outputs back lets them blend smoothly over time.
4|One **neuron** takes a vector **x**, weights it by **w** and sums into one number **y**. >> **Vector in, one number out**, an inner product. || A **network** of 4, 3, 4 and 5 units uses **W₁, W₂ and W₃**. >> Each W maps one vector to the next. >> The last layer is a **decision vector**, so it is **vector in, decision vector out**. || An **LLM** passes **vectors 1 through 15** up through **5 layers** of W blocks. >> Six colors mark six kinds of weight sets. >> **Vector 16** comes out as a **PDF**, a probability distribution over the next token. >> It is **a dynamical system with many vectors in and one decision vector out**. || An **agentic system is a graph of LLMs**. >> **Outline, Write, Critic, Revise and Final** LLMs run in order. >> A **loop** between Critic and Revise sends work back for revision. || Two operations run under every step. >> **Inference is inner products.** >> **Learning is SGD plus DP**, gradient descent organized by backpropagation.
5|**15 vectors**, **x₁ to x₁₅**, and an empty **x₁₆** raise the question **vectors of what?** >> An LLM reads a sequence of vectors and predicts the next one. || **A sentence cut into 15 words**. >> “The cat that the dog chased ran up the old tree and sat on the ___.” >> **x₁₆ is the missing word**, which depends on words far back. || **A spectrogram cut into 15 frames** and **an image cut into 16 patches**. >> The spectrogram spans 0 to 5 kHz over time, and **x₁₆ is the next frame**. >> The image is read row by row, and **x₁₆ is the missing bottom-right patch**. || **A protein, the insulin B chain**, read as **15 amino acids**. >> F V N Q H L C G S H L V E A L fold into a helix, and **x₁₆ is the next residue, Y**. || **The LLM only ever sees vectors.** >> Words, sounds, pixels and molecules become the same kind of input. >> **One machine asks one question, what is x₁₆?**
6|**Which sequences can be English words?** >> Twenty **one-syllable words** like Don, mat, cup and fish each have one consonant, one vowel and one consonant. >> **A simple syllable in English can have a consonant, then a vowel, then a consonant.** || **Clusters** come as **onsets** before the vowel and **codas** after it. >> Onsets appear in split, flat, black, glad and stop. >> Codas appear in desk, box, help, milk and lamp. >> Each has a starred impossible twin, such as *sblit, *lfat, *desb and *hepl. || Order matters. >> flat and *lfat, help and *hepl, lamp and *lapm use the same letters in different orders. >> The allowed order mirrors around the vowel. >> **Only certain sequences are allowed before the vowel and after the vowel.** || Long words have several syllables. >> At·lan·tic, ath·lete and ad·mit hold t·l, th·l and d·m across breaks. >> Those pairs cannot start a word, so *tlantic, *thlete and *dmit are impossible. >> ng ends a syllable, as in ring, but never starts one, so *ngapkin is impossible. || **A long word is a chain of syllables**, each with the same rules. >> **t·l is fine across a break, but tl cannot start a syllable.** >> This knowledge is **phonotactics**, known without being taught.
7|The quiz reveals **phonotactic knowledge through GPT learning**. >> Each item starts an English word, and the task is to **guess the next letter**. >> People agree quickly, and agreement varies by item. || After a vowel, almost anything fits. >> After **ra_**, t, n, r and l are all likely. || At a word start, choices narrow. >> **sp_**, **b_** and **f_** take **l, r or a vowel**, never *spn, *bn or *fn. >> **k_** takes a vowel or **n**, as in knee, never *kt. >> **sh_** takes a vowel or **r**, as in shrimp, never *shb. || Some starts allow only vowels. >> **skr_** and **m_** take only a vowel, never *skrl or *mb. >> **bram_** nearly forces b, as in bramble, unless a new syllable starts. || **Guessing the next letter is predicting x₁₆.** >> A **GPT trained only on this game learns English sounds and spelling**. >> Its distribution is flat after fa_ and sharply peaked after skr_.
