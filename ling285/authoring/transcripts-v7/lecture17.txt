2|The vowel network returns as a picture of what neural networks did from **1943 to 1986**. >> Two formants come in, weighted connections carry them through three hidden layers, and five outputs respond for done, dune, dean, Don and Dan. >> The weights are the same illustrative signed weights used for forward propagation and backpropagation. || **McCulloch and Pitts (1943)** mark the start. >> Their paper is **“A Logical Calculus of the Ideas Immanent in Nervous Activity.”** >> It begins from the **“all-or-none” character of nervous activity**, a neuron either fires or not, and treats neural events with propositional logic. || The two **Parallel Distributed Processing** volumes of **1986** mark the end. >> They made networks of many simple connected units, trained by adjusting weights, widely used. || **Given some static vector of formants, the NN could make a decision about a few possible categories.** >> A static vector is one fixed list of numbers measured at one moment, here F1 and F2. >> The network maps that single input onto one of five words. || **Problem. Human cognition is far more complex.** >> Speech unfolds in time, and a sentence is a long sequence in which what comes next depends on what came before. >> A network that maps one fixed vector to a few labels cannot keep track of earlier inputs, which is the gap recurrent networks and then LLMs fill.
3|In **1985, Michael Jordan developed recurrent neural networks** to make sense of **speech production**. >> That work led toward **LLMs** and then **agentic AI**. >> The photo on the right is Michael Jordan. || The network has four kinds of units. >> **Plan units** take outside input that says which sequence to produce. >> **Hidden units** combine information, and **output units** give the response. >> **State units** receive the outputs back along the long curved arrows, and each state unit also feeds back to itself. || Those loops make the network **recurrent**. >> The state units hold a trace of what the network just produced. >> That trace feeds the hidden units along with the plan, so each output depends on the plan and on the network’s own recent outputs. >> A feedforward classifier has no such memory, since each input passes through once. || The plot, **Performance on /istrstry/**, tracks the output over 32 time steps. >> Each row is one articulatory feature, **Voice, Place, Sonorant, Sibilant, Nasal, Height, Back and Round**. >> Sibilant rises in bumps where the s sounds occur, while Nasal and Height stay nearly flat. || **Round** rises early and stays high for most of the sequence. >> Instead of switching on and off for each sound, the feature spreads across neighbors. >> Feeding outputs back makes successive outputs blend smoothly, and that idea of using past outputs carries on into today’s language models.
4|The page builds from **one neuron** to an **agentic system** in four steps. >> A vector **x** comes into one neuron, each part is multiplied by a weight **w**, and the products are summed into one number **y**. >> **Vector in, one number out**, and that sum is an inner product. || A **network** stacks layers of 4, 3, 4 and 5 units joined by **W₁, W₂ and W₃**. >> Each W turns one vector into the next. >> The last layer is a **decision vector**, one value per possible answer, so this is **vector in, decision vector out**, like the vowel classifier. || An **LLM** takes **vectors 1 through 15** in at the bottom, one per token, and passes them up through **5 layers** of W blocks. >> The six colors of W stand for six different kinds of weight sets. >> At the top comes **vector 16**, drawn as a bar chart labeled **PDF**, a probability distribution over the next token. >> It is called **a dynamical system with many vectors in and one decision vector out**. || An **agentic system is a graph of LLMs**. >> Separate LLMs run in order, **Outline, Write, Critic, Revise and Final**. >> A **loop** between Critic and Revise lets the critique send work back for more revision before the final model finishes. || The bottom line names the two operations under every step. >> **Inference is inner products**, weighted sums passed from layer to layer. >> **Learning is SGD plus DP**, gradient descent driven by the dynamic-programming bookkeeping of backpropagation.
5|The page starts with **15 vectors, x₁ to x₁₅**, an empty slot for **x₁₆**, and the question **vectors of what?** >> An LLM receives a sequence of vectors and predicts the next one. >> Four reveals fill the same arrangement with four kinds of sequence. || First, **a sentence cut into 15 words**. >> “The cat that the dog chased ran up the old tree and sat on the ___.” >> Each vector stands for one word, and **x₁₆ is the missing word**. >> Filling it in needs words far back in the sentence, since the cat is the one sitting. || Next, **a spectrogram cut into 15 frames**, then **an image cut into 16 patches**. >> The spectrogram runs from 0 to 5 kHz up the side with time to the right, and **x₁₆ is the next frame**. >> The image of a house, a tree and the sun is read row by row, and **x₁₆ is the missing bottom-right patch**. || Last, **a protein, the insulin B chain**, read as **15 amino acids**. >> The letters are F V N Q H L C G S H L V E A L, drawn folding into a helix. >> **x₁₆ is the next residue**, which is **Y**. || **The LLM only ever sees vectors.** >> Words, sounds, pixels and molecules all become sequences of vectors. >> So **one machine asks one question, what is x₁₆?**, whatever the source of the sequence.
6|The page asks **which sequences can be English words**, in three rows. >> Row one has twenty **one-syllable words**, each a single consonant, a single vowel and a single consonant, such as Don, mat, sit, cup, fish and ring. >> Consonants are dark, vowels red, and a silent e grey. >> **A simple syllable in English can have a consonant, then a vowel, then a consonant.** || Row two adds **clusters**, groups of consonants. >> Clusters before the vowel are **onsets**, as in split, flat, black, glad and stop. >> Clusters after the vowel are **codas**, as in desk, box, help, milk and lamp. >> Each real word has a starred impossible twin, such as *sblit, *lfat, *bnack, *dlad, *tsop, *desb, *bokp, *hepl, *mikl and *lapm. || Many twins use the same letters in the other order. >> flat and *lfat, help and *hepl, lamp and *lapm differ only in order. >> **Order matters**, and the pattern mirrors around the vowel. >> **Only certain sequences are allowed before the vowel and after the vowel.** || Row three has **words of many syllables**, with dots marking the breaks. >> ba·na·na, win·dow, pump·kin, nap·kin, At·lan·tic, ath·lete, ad·mit and spa·ghet·ti are real words. >> t then l, th then l, and d then m are fine across a break, but cannot start a word, so *tlantic, *thlete and *dmit are impossible. >> ng can end a syllable, as in ring, but never start one, so *ngapkin is impossible. || **A long word is a chain of syllables**, each obeying the same rules. >> **t·l is fine across a break, but tl cannot start a syllable.** >> This knowledge of allowed sound sequences is **phonotactics**, and English speakers have it without being taught.
7|The pop quiz asks how **phonotactic knowledge can be revealed through GPT learning**. >> Each item is the start of an English word, and the task is to **guess the next letter**. >> The starts are ra_, sp_, k_, skr_, m_, bram_, b_, f_ and sh_. >> People agree quickly, and how much they agree changes from item to item. || After a vowel the choices open up. >> After **ra_**, letters like t, n, r and l are all likely, since **after a vowel almost anything** can follow. || At the start of a word the choices narrow. >> After **sp_**, **b_** and **f_**, only **l, r or a vowel**, never *spn, *bn or *fn. >> After **k_**, a vowel or **n** as in knee, never *kt. >> After **sh_**, a vowel or **r** as in shrimp, never *shb. || Some starts leave almost no choice. >> After **skr_** and after **m_**, only a vowel, never *skrl or *mb. >> After **bram_**, b is nearly forced, as in bramble, though a new syllable could also start there. || **Guessing the next letter is predicting x₁₆.** >> A **GPT trained only on this game learns English sounds and spelling**, because it must match these patterns. >> Its predicted distribution is flat after fa_ and sharply peaked after skr_.
