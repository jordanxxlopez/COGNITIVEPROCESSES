# LING285 source and teaching requirements

These requirements come directly from the user and apply to all LING285 authoring and revisions. Follow the repository's study-writing rules as well.

## Process sources before writing teaching material

For every uploaded PDF, PowerPoint, and other source, extract all machine-readable text from every page, including titles, bullets, captions, labels, annotations, tables, footnotes, references, linguistic examples, transcriptions, glosses, equations, and text within figures. Preserve speaker notes and document equations. OCR image text and content that ordinary extraction misses.

Render and inspect every page at a resolution adequate for small labels and visual details. Extract and inspect embedded images individually whenever possible. Inspect animated media in addition to static exports, which can conceal video content or flatten animation stages. Build a page-by-page inventory before authoring. Record missing, unreadable, or unreviewed sources honestly. Do not claim a static thumbnail inspection establishes full animation or audio coverage.

Inspect linguistic examples, phonetic transcriptions, waveforms, spectrograms, articulatory and acoustic diagrams, phonological representations, graphs, charts, tables, models, experiments, physical systems, mathematical relationships, algorithms, neural networks, architectures, flowcharts, code, and labeled comparisons. Read their labels, legends, axes, units, color keys, symbols, brackets, slashes, diacritics, arrows, captions, and callouts.

In teaching notes, incorporate information communicated visually even when absent from nearby prose. Explain stages in their depicted order, comparisons and relationships, graph variables and results, meaningful table rows and columns, and experiment purposes, setups, results, and source-supported conclusions. Explain code purposes, inputs, operations, variables, functions, and outputs when established by the source. Integrate continuing figures without unnecessary repetition.

Use surrounding text and captions to interpret visuals. Do not invent missing details. Keep original visuals rather than generating replacement images. Preserve source documents verbatim and distinguish source text from authored teaching. Before completing an output, compare its notes with the corresponding pages and inventory. Retain source references for verification.

## Iskarous's teaching and exam style

Teach through intuition and cause and effect in plain language. Explain what moves, what changes, why it changes, and what that causes naturally where the concept calls for it. Avoid imposing a repeated template on every section.

When a source builds from a simple case to a complex one, explain how each step extends the previous one. Retain source-supported everyday applications, including music, buildings, hearing, speech, and AI. Make shared principles across systems explicit when supported, such as feedback in springs, thermostats, and learning.

For a prediction or demonstration, explain the prediction, what was measured, and the supported outcome. Include an equation only when visibly present in a source. Explain its meaning and how behavior changes instead of turning the lesson into a calculation exercise.

Practice should emphasize short conceptual explanations consistent with the uploaded Exam 1 Review. Do not transfer PSYC301's researcher-name-heavy multiple-choice format to this course by default. Treat generated practice and answer explanations as study aids, not actual instructor exam questions or guaranteed predictions.

The user's subsequent revision explicitly requests both A–D multiple-choice questions and the existing written prompts. Use the PSYC301 practice interaction and substantial, varied distractors while grounding LING285 content and reasoning in the supplied review. Explain every answer option. Keep observed short-answer exam evidence distinct from generated MCQ adaptations. Document a reproducible study-relevance model without calling its scores validated exam probabilities.
